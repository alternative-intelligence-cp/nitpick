# 1.5.6c — What the floor's spec ASSUMES of its callers: two false clauses, a form for ranges that may alias, and the assumptions said out loud (leads E-1, E-2)

**Planned execution-grade 2026-09-17 on 1.5.6b's close by `nitpick-compiler_s7`. APPROVED the same day: the
proposal went to the user in plain text while 1.5.6b's harnesses ran — the two false clauses found, the fixes
measured, "its own subcycle, before 1.5.7's plan" — and the answer, in this seat's own session, was "go with your
recommendations on all three" (S-75, S-76, and this). No question is open: every choice below is a choice about the
floor's EVIDENCE (the spec's grammar, the translator, a generated region of TCB.md), none about the language, and
each is settled here with its reason (§5). The floor's BYTES do not move in this subcycle; if a step finds that they
must, it stops and the library listener gets both digests first.**

Why this subcycle exists. 1.5.6 gave the floor a specification and a translator, and at the s6→s7 hand-off the
outgoing seat named four places it would try to prove that evidence wrong. 1.5.6b took E-3 and E-4. E-1 and E-2 are
the same question asked of the spec itself: **a section's `requires` and `(objects …)` clauses are HYPOTHESES of its
rows — so who proves them?** A translated caller of a `(summary)` symbol does, as rows at the call. Nobody else
does: a caller that is a boundary symbol, a symbol with no section, or a call the EMITTER generates is checked by
nothing. That is sound as designed (D-288: a boundary is a promise) and it READS stronger than it is — and a first
walk, made while 1.5.6b's harnesses ran, found that two of the hypotheses are not merely unproven but FALSE for
legal callers. 1.5.7's explorer stands on this evidence, so it is put right first (the reason E-4 went first).

## 1. Where it starts (measured 2026-09-17 on 1.5.6b's tree; the pinned z3 under the profile)

  empty range is no object), asserted as hypotheses of the section's rows and RECORDED (`t.obj_lo`/`t.obj_len`) for
  four uses (`objects_facts`, `npkg/floor_smt.npk:1332`): `caller_objects_in` (what a callee's `(frame objects)`

  symbol declares objects today, so that arm has never run). `(frame objects)` in a section's own frame row reads the
  clause a second way (`objects_in`, ~3482).
- **SIX CLAUSE HEADS ARE READ ONCE AND A SECOND IS DROPPED IN SILENCE**: `spec_clause(s, sec, HEAD, 0)` for

## 2. The design

### 2.1 `(lo len apart-when COND)`: an object set apart only where the body needs it
A range entry of an `(objects …)` clause may carry a condition over the entry state: the range **lies in the
address space ALWAYS** (its bounds fact is unconditional — true of a list word, which is null or a chunk — and that
is what keeps it RECORDED as an object is, so a sum inside it does not wrap and the body's text stays what it was)
and is **set apart from the section's other objects only where COND holds** (each pair fact gains `(not COND)` as
a disjunct; a pair of two such ranges, both). Under `(frame objects)` it counts among the symbol's objects where
COND holds. Section-level only (a loop's `(objects …)` keeps `(lo len)`: `objects_ranges` refuses the longer entry).
`npk_small_free`'s three list ranges become `(<lo> 65536 apart-when (= (load64 mem (+ (- ip (mod ip 65536)) 24)) 0))`
— the chunk's free count at ENTRY is zero exactly when `topart` runs. The section's comment says what the clause
was, why it was false and for which frees.
**Implementation:** `range_entry_ok` and the `whens` list in `objects_facts`, the conjunct in `objects_in`
(`npkg/floor_smt.npk`); every section without the form emits BYTE-IDENTICAL text (measured: 105 of 106 files).
Self-check, both runners, one pair of texts through the built tool: `floor-apart-when` (a claim that needs the
apartness everywhere is refuted) and `floor-apart-when-control` (the same claim under COND discharges); a malformed
entry is refused by name by the translator. `runtime/npkrt.obligations` re-recorded: 13 hashes, no verdict (THE
GATE: the complete diff read). TCB.md regenerated (the disposition does not move: 7 discharged, 6 residue).
VERIFICATION_REFERENCE §9's `(objects …)` row gains the form and its reason.

### 2.2 `(views (lo len) …)` — ranges a symbol is handed to READ, which may overlap one another
A new section-level clause beside `(objects …)`, named with the language's own word for a borrowed read-only window
(D-186, D-249, D-266). Each range: inside the address space; APART from every `(objects …)` range of the section;
**not asserted apart from the other views** (two views of one string); recorded exactly as an object is, for all
four uses of §1. **And READ-ONLY IS PROVEN, not named**: the section's frame row may exempt no byte of a view —
with `vin` = "the point lies in a view", the row's guard `(not inr)` becomes `(or (not inr) vin)`, and
`objects_in` (the `(frame objects)` reading) counts the views among what the symbol was handed; a section with no
frame clause already claims every byte unchanged. So every section with views has a row that fails if the body
writes one. (A name that claims a property nothing decides is the toy model of 1.5.6b step 4d again.)
Dropping the view/view apartness can only make a row HARDER to prove, never unsound; the liveness of each range —
that it is allocated, static or stack memory, which is what makes the allocator's fresh block apart from it — is
the caller's to keep, as it is for an object (§2.4's acceptance says so).
`npk_string_concat` becomes `(views (a.0 a.1) (b.0 b.1))` under `(frame objects)`; its 12 rows are re-decided
(expected: 12 `unsat`, as the deletion measured) and re-recorded. A section with ONE range keeps `(objects …)`:
with one range the two clauses emit the same text.
**Implementation** (`npkg/floor_smt.npk`; the floor tool is built by the SNAPSHOT — write it in the module's own
conservative style): `objects_facts` takes the views clause beside the objects clause (`-1` for none) and renders
both lists ONCE: the objects' facts first and unchanged — so every section without views emits BYTE-IDENTICAL text
and no hash but `npk_string_concat`'s moves — then per view its bounds fact and one `(or no_obj apart)` per object,
recording both kinds. Its three callers pass the clause: the section's (~4747), a summary call's (~3304: the facts
are rows there, as objects' are — free by construction, exercised by no symbol today, as the objects arm is not),
and a loop's (~1576: `-1` always — a loop clause takes no views). `objects_in` appends the views' ranges;
`views_in` is its sibling; `tx_section_rows` (~4460) builds the guard. The summary CALL side's frame template is
NOT widened (a caller assumes `¬inr ⇒ unchanged`, which the callee's row implies): sound, and less than it could
assume.
**Grammar, both runners** (`floor.py` `_CLAUSE_HEADS` and `check_spec`; `npkg/floor.npk` ~283): `views` is known;
**a head the translator reads once may be written once** — `objects`, `views`, `frame`, `ensures-trap`,
`ensures-fresh`, `residue`, `boundary`, `summary` — a second is refused by name ("a clause read once and written
twice in …"); **a `(loop …)`'s sub-clauses are `invariant`, `unroll`, `objects`, `inst`** — another head is refused
("a loop sub-clause the grammar does not know …"), a second `objects` a duplicate.
**Self-check, both runners, ONE set of texts**, through the built floor tool over a synthetic root as
`floor-refuted` is: `floor-views-written` (a body that stores through a view under `(frame (p 8))`: the frame row
is REFUTED, must fail), `floor-views-control` (the same body with `p` an object and `q` the view: passes),
`floor-views-alias` (`(ensures (=> (and (not (= p 0)) (not (= q 0))) (not (= p q))))` under `(views (p 8) (q 8))`:
refuted, must fail — views MAY alias), `floor-objects-apart-control` (the same claim under `(objects …)`: passes);
and at the grammar: `floor-spec-duplicate` (+ control), `floor-spec-loop-clause` (+ control).
VERIFICATION_REFERENCE §9's grammar gains the clause, the two refusals, and the sentence that `(objects …)`,
`(views …)` and `requires` are HYPOTHESES about the caller.

### 2.3 Every other apartness, ARGUED where it is assumed
The eleven multi-object sections of §1 each gain a comment: per pair, per caller, why the ranges cannot coincide —
the invariant named (a frame in at most one of three places; unlink-before-link under the list lock; a chunk on one
list; linear lists; the arena value vs a block never handed out). Comments only: the rows' hashes are over the
canonical SMT text and do not move — the gate is `npkg verify --floor-only` WITHOUT `--record` answering "matches".
A pair that, on the closer read this step makes, CAN coincide is a finding: stop, measure, fix as §2.1 was.

### 2.4 The assumptions said out loud: TCB.md §4d, GENERATED
A fourth generated region, `floor-callers`: one row per section that has rows and a caller assumption —
`| symbol | assumes | a row at the call | inlined into | NOT PROVED: floor callers | NOT PROVED: emitted code |` —
"assumes" lists which of `requires`/`objects`/`views` the section carries; "a row at the call" the translated
callers of a `(summary)` symbol; "inlined into" the translated callers of a non-summary symbol (the caller's rows
cover the body in the caller's own context); the fifth column every floor caller that is NOT translated (a boundary
symbol, a symbol with no claims: nothing proves the assumption at that call); the last `yes` when the symbol is
EXPORTED — its `define` is not `internal`/`private`, so emitted code (the emitter's calls, LLVM's own lowering)
can reach it and nothing proves the assumption there either. Both generators (`floor.py` + `tcb_floor.py --write`;
`npkg/floor.npk`), the currency belt in both runners, as the three regions of 1.5.6 are held; the twins held to
each other byte for byte before wiring. **§5 gains its sixteenth acceptance**: that a section's `requires`,
`(objects …)` and `(views …)` — the ranges' LIVENESS included — are its CALLERS' to keep; that §4d says for which
callers a row keeps them and for which nothing does; that the structural ones are argued in the spec beside the
clause; and that 1.5.7's explorer can carry them as EXECUTABLE assertions in its mock floor, which is where an
assumption of this kind gets tested rather than argued.

## 5. Decisions taken at planning
- **M-1 — a new clause, not a reading of `requires`.** A bare `requires` gives the address-space bound and nothing
  else; the rows need the RECORDING (freshness, no-wrap). `(views …)` is `(objects …)` minus one family of pairs,
  plus a proof obligation.
- **M-2 — views are apart from objects, not from each other.** A symbol whose read and written ranges may
  legitimately overlap (`memmove`) states neither clause and keeps its `requires`, as today.
- **M-3 — read-only is PROVEN by the frame row** (reversed at planning from "no check": a clause named for a
  property nothing decides is the defect 1.5.6b step 4d found in its own self-check).
- **M-4 — `npk_small_free` is fixed by CONDITIONING THE APARTNESS, not by splitting the symbol or un-inlining
  `npk_ch_unlink`/`npk_ch_push`** (1.5.6 step 4 inlined them because their frames as templates cost 31 rows over
  2,096 hypotheses each) — **and the condition is a form of the grammar, `apart-when`, not an `ite` inside the
  range.** The `ite` spellings say the same thing and need no translator change, and they take the file from 202 s
  to 357–908 s against a 250 s hang net (§1): the null-or-empty range reaches every bounds fact and every pair, and
  it breaks the spelled match that keeps a sum inside a declared object from wrapping. `apart-when` leaves all of
  that alone and weakens the pair facts only: 194 s.
- **M-4b — the hang net is NOT raised by this subcycle; the margin it found is S-77, the user's.** `120 +
  10·checks` leaves `npk_small_free` (six rows that burn the whole budget) at 194–203 s of its 250 on this machine
  under four concurrent harnesses — 81% of the net, where the next file stands at 20% (`npk_hs_put_dec`, 69 s of
  350) and every other under 10% (measured over every floor file in the runner's own mode; the step-0 record carries
  the table). It has passed every run, and a machine a quarter slower, or a fifth and sixth concurrent harness,
  would trip it: a red by P-13 that is no verdict. Raising a safety instrument's bound to fit one's own step is the
  wrong order, so the step was made to fit instead (M-4) and the question is raised on its own: OPEN_DECISIONS
  S-77, with the recommendation — a net that knows which rows the committed manifest records as `budget`, both
  runners.
- **M-5 — E-2's sentence is a GENERATED TABLE.** A hand-written sentence about who keeps 31 sections' assumptions
  is the next stale list (the lesson of 1.5.6b step 1's generated head that claimed rows `clone` and `execve` did
  not have).
- **M-6 — "emitted code can call it" is the floor's own `define` line (exported or not), never a scan of the
  emitter.** A scan misses `memcpy`/`memset`/`memmove` (LLVM's lowering emits those calls) and hits a comment
  (`npk_small_free`, which is `internal`). Exported is the conservative, structural reading (DEF-21's allowlist is
  the same set).
- **M-7 — the two exported symbols nobody outside calls (`npk_rq_pop`, `npk_frame_drain`) stay exported in this
  subcycle.** Narrowing one moves the floor's bytes for no behaviour; OWNER: the next step that moves the floor for
  a reason of its own reads §4d's last column and narrows them in the same digest move (recorded in the close).
- **M-8 — views at a summary call are supported, not refused**: the facts are one function's output at all three
  sites, so supporting them costs one argument; refusing would be a second code path to keep true.
