## 1. Where you are

(D-205: `src/` carries no contract, no `old`, no `result`, so nothing here

**What exists.** A function's contracts are `VerifyContractNode`s in its
declaration window (`fn_contract(ast, d, i)`, `fn_contract_count`; the kind
`contract_kind(v)` is `CONTRACT_REQUIRES`/`CONTRACT_ENSURES`, the condition

fails` function, `raw f(…)`, or a builtin bare by its columns), types
`result` (`ExprResultValueExpr`, a leaf) as `fn_success` in `ensures` only and
`old(expr)` (`ExprOldExpr`, operand in `a`) as its operand in `ensures` and
`invariant`, copyable only. `main` and `failsafe` carry no contract
(D-244). Macro expansion clones verify nodes (V-19).

### Step 1 — the checks in every build (the emitter)

**1a. The three codes and the check shape.** `emit_contract_check(x, cond,
code, space, site, at)` in `ir_rules.npk`: `emit_cond` over the contract
expression (the statement's temporaries dropped, `fnem_temp_mark`/
`emit_temp_drops`/`fnem_temp_release` as `emit_rule_fn`'s clauses do), a
branch to `ctr.bad` (`npk_chain_reset(<x_site>)`, `npk_trap(i32 <code>)`,
`unreachable`) or `ctr.ok`; ELIDED when `llctx_elided(space, site, kind)`
says the row is discharged — no assume (L-11). One check per clause
(`requires`, `invariant`) or per return point (`ensures`, all clauses
`and`-chained into one branch — L-13's accounting).

**1b. `requires` at the entry, as a predicate.** A function with `requires`
clauses gets a PREDICATE FUNCTION `<sym>.req` (`req_sym`, the `.req` inside
a quoted symbol as `body_sym` places `.body`): `i8` over the function's
parameter list (the same `fnem_param` bindings, `move` qualifiers
included — the predicate borrows), its body the clauses through `emit_cond`
in order, each false clause its own `ret i8 0` (the check emitter traps
with the CLAUSE's site so the origin chain names the clause), `ret i8 1` at
the end — `emit_rule_fn` is the template line for line, `emit_req_fn(x,
did, sym)` beside it. Emitted from `emit_function` before the body, and the
walk (`obl_function`) runs over the FUNCTION, not the predicate (the
predicate has no rows of its own: the clauses' own guards — a division in a
`requires` — are recorded as the function's SITE_DECL row's business, L-5).
The check: `emit_checked_entry` (sync, split) calls `<sym>.req` with the
`%a` registers after the limit checks and traps −4112 on 0; a coroutine
calls it at state 0 with the loaded parameter slots after the limit entry
checks (the loop in `emit_function`). The `split` decision reads "limited
parameter OR a `requires`" — a sync function with a `requires` and no
limited parameter splits too, so D-252's bypass serves it.
`callee_bypasses` (D-252) answers true only when EVERY bypass row at the
call is discharged: the `limit-subsume` row when the callee has limited
parameters AND the call-site `requires` row when it has clauses (both kinds
through `llctx_elided` at `SITE_EXPR`, the call). The in-place elision of
the entry check reads the function's SITE_DECL `requires` row (step 2).

**1c. `old` snapshots.** At the body's start — after the entry checks, in
`.body` for a split function — every `old(...)` node in the function's
`ensures` clauses and in every invariant of its body is evaluated ONCE and
stored: `collect_old(x, d, body)` walks the contract expressions and the
body's loop invariants in source order (`expr_children`'s order, the
encoder's) and lists the `ExprOldExpr` ids; each gets a slot — sync: a
hoisted alloca (`irw_alloca`); coroutine: a frame crossing at **role 60 +
ordinal on the function BODY's block statement** (`scan_crossing` registers
them; `rg -n "60i32" src/backend/ir/` finds no such role today) — held in
`FnEmitter.old_ids`/`old_slots` (parallel lists, reset in `fnem_begin`). The
`ExprOldExpr` arm of `emit_expr_kind` loads its slot (the rung retires); an
`old` whose id is not in the table is `iv_broken` (a defect, never a rung).

**1d. `ensures` at the return seam.** In `emit_pass`, after `emit_fit` gives
the value in register and BEFORE `fnem_ret_pass`: `x.fe.result_ref` set to
the register (the `ExprResultValueExpr` arm reads it; the rung retires), one
`emit_contract_check` over the conjunction of the `ensures` clauses
(SITE_STMT, the `pass` statement, code −4113), `result_ref` cleared after.
A `pass` with no operand (`NIL` success) checks the clauses with
`result_ref` empty (TYPE-060 keeps `result` out of a `NIL` function's
`ensures`? — it does not; `result` there is `NIL`, a leaf the emitter
answers with `iv_nil`). `emit_return` (the long form): after `emit_expr`,
the aggregate's error field extracted; on `0` the value field is `result`
and the check runs, on nonzero nothing (the postcondition is the SUCCESS
value's, D-221). `fail`/`relay` check nothing. Bare functions carry no
contract except `failsafe`'s injected one: `emit_exit` in `failsafe`
(`FnEmitter.is_failsafe`, set in `emit_function` from the declaration's
name) checks `<code> > 0` — the guard S-43 recommends — with −4113 (an
`ensures` that is the compiler's), SITE_STMT the `exit`; the trap route's
re-entry rule turns it into exit 70 (VERIFICATION §4.6).

**1e. `invariant` at the loop head.** In `emit_while`, `emit_when` (before
the condition at `head_l`), `emit_for` (at `fr.head`, before the compare)
and `emit_counted` (at its head, after `fnem_loop_push` so `$` reads the
counter slot): one `emit_contract_check` per clause (SITE_STMT the LOOP
statement, code −4114); the four `iv_rung` sites retire;
`BACKEND_CARRIER_READS["stmt_while_invariant"]` says "read at the loop
head". The head runs on entry and after every iteration, which is D-221's
"holds at entry and is preserved" at runtime; the elision reads BOTH rows of
the loop (L-8).

**1f. Tests.** The three rung files leave `tests/rejection/` (the README's

### Step 2 — the rows, the hypotheses, the elision (the encoder)

**2a. Substitution.** `enc_under(e, names, terms, expr) → Term` in
`smt_encode.npk`: `scope_mark`; for each pair `bind_define(name, ty, term,
0)` (a fresh version equal to the term — the existing mechanism, so a
compound argument term is named once); `enc_expr(expr)`; `scope_release`.
`Encoder.result` (a Term, like `subject`) is what the `ExprResultValueExpr`
arm returns (opaque outside a binding); `Encoder.at_entry` makes the
identifier arm answer a binding's ENTRY symbol (`Bind.entry_sym`, recorded
by `enc_param`) for the `ExprOldExpr` arm, which encodes its operand with
`at_entry` set (a non-parameter name inside is refused at step 0, so every
name has an entry symbol).

**2b. A call's `requires` row and `ensures` hypothesis.** In the call arm of
`enc_expr` beside `record_subsume`: `record_requires(e, id, n)` — the
callee the checker recorded (`exprtypes_callee`, the trait's method for a
`dyn` or bound call, or the identifier's declaration for a free call), its
`requires` clauses conjoined under `enc_under(params ↦ argument terms)`, ONE
row per call (kind `requires`, SITE_EXPR, the call), `unencoded` when a
parameter's type is outside the fragment; an `async`/`thread` callee's call
records the row too (S-44) — the guard it names is the entry check, never
bypassed there, so the word is `retained`. Then the value: a call to a
`pure never fails` function encodes as an UNINTERPRETED FUNCTION
application `(|<sym>| args…)` (L-3): `sym_new_fun(e, sym, arg_sorts,
ret_sort)` in the symbol table with an arity, `enc_finish` declaring it as
`(declare-fun |<sym>| (Int …) Int)` when it is in a cone (the cone closure
already walks term symbols; a function symbol rides in `Term.syms` like a
constant); an argument outside the fragment makes the application opaque. A
call to anything else stays opaque. THE CALLEE'S `ensures` AS KNOWLEDGE: at
an unwrap that continues only on success — `raw f(…)` (never fails),
`f(…) ?! E`, `relay f(…)`, and the same three over `await f(…)` — the
unwrap's value gets a fresh symbol `v` and the callee's `ensures` clauses
are pushed as a hypothesis under `enc_under(params ↦ args, result ↦ v)`;
at a UF application the same hypothesis is pushed with `result ↦ (|f|
args)`, once per application. `?|` pushes nothing (its value may be the
default). Sound because the callee's `ensures` is guarded at its own seam or
proven there (P-8's shape, L-4).

**2c. The function's own rows.** `enc_param` records the entry `requires`
row (kind `requires`, SITE_DECL, the FUNCTION's declaration index; the
conjunction over the parameters' first versions, hypotheses the range
axioms) — discharged, the entry check is elided in place; then pushes the
`requires` conjunction as a hypothesis (the body may assume its
precondition, exactly as a limited parameter's rule is assumed after its
entry row — `assert_rule`'s order). At every `pass v` and `return
Result{…}` (`walk_stmt`'s return arm): kind `ensures`, SITE_STMT the
statement, goal the conjunction under `result ↦ enc_expr(v)` (a `pass`
with no operand: `result` opaque); the long form only when the literal's
error field encodes to `0`. In `failsafe` (`e.is_failsafe`): at every
`exit v`, kind `failsafe-post`, SITE_STMT, goal `(> v 0)` — S-43's guard
elides on discharge.

**2d. Loop rows.** In the `while`/`when`/`for`/counted arms: (1) the ENTRY
row — kind `invariant`, SITE_STMT the loop statement, goal the conjunction
over the CURRENT versions, before any havoc; (2) `walk_loop_body`'s havoc of
the assigned names; (3) inside the body's region: the conjunction pushed as
a hypothesis over the havoced versions, and for `while`/`when` the encoded
condition too (both dominate only the body); (4) at the body's END the
PRESERVATION row — kind `invariant`, SITE_STMT the body BLOCK's statement
index, goal the conjunction over the versions at the end — and at every
`continue` in the body (a head visit) another preservation row keyed at the
`continue` statement; (5) after the loop: the assigned names havoced again
and, when the body holds no `break`, the conjunction pushed as a hypothesis
(it holds at every head visit and the exit is a head visit); with a
`break`, nothing. The condition's negation is NOT pushed after the loop —
that is 1.5.4's path condition (L-9). A counted loop's `$` is opaque outside
a rule (1.5.2 L-19), so an invariant naming it is `open` until 1.5.4 gives
it a term; recorded, never refused. `for` binds its loop variable opaque, as
today.

**2e. Conformance.** In `obl_function` for an impl METHOD whose trait
method carries contracts (the impl's declaration reached through
`impl_target`/the checker's method table; the trait method's declaration
its counterpart by name): after the parameters are bound, `region_enter`;
the trait's `requires` pushed as a hypothesis under `enc_under(trait params
↦ impl parameter terms, by position)`; kind `requires`, SITE_DECL the IMPL
method's declaration, goal the impl's own `requires` conjunction ("an impl
may weaken `requires`": trait ⇒ impl); `region_leave`. Then `region_enter`;
a fresh symbol `r`; the impl's `ensures` pushed under `result ↦ r`; kind
`ensures`, SITE_DECL the impl method, goal the trait's `ensures` under
`result ↦ r` ("may strengthen `ensures`": impl ⇒ trait); `region_leave`.
Neither row has a guard of its own (the impl's own entry and seam checks
are the runtime), so their elision word is `none`; an open row is REPORTED,
never a refusal (L-6).

**2f. Elision and the accounting.** `enc_site_discharged` answers the
emitter for (SITE_DECL, function, `requires`), (SITE_STMT, pass/return,
`ensures`), (SITE_STMT, loop and body and continue, `invariant` — the head
check elides only when EVERY row of the loop is discharged; `loop_rows_
discharged(x, sid)` collects them), (SITE_STMT, exit, `failsafe-post`),
(SITE_EXPR, call, `requires` — the bypass). `rows.txt` (the `--obligations`
artefact) gains a seventh column `<space>:<site>` so both runners' belts can
count SITES: `.body` calls == the number of distinct call sites whose every
bypass-kind row (`limit-subsume`, `requires`) is discharged and that carry at
least one; `-4112` traps == `requires` clauses of functions whose SITE_DECL
row is retained (one trap per clause in the predicate); `-4113` traps ==
retained `ensures` rows + retained `failsafe-post` rows (one check per
return point or exit); `-4114` traps == clauses of loops with any retained
`invariant` row (one trap per clause at the head). `npkg verify`'s
`OV_*`/elision words are already kind-aware; the `none` word extends to the
conformance rows by their SITE_DECL-on-an-impl key (a `kind`+`space` test in
`man_row`'s caller).

## 3. Decisions taken at planning

- **L-1 — `requires` is checked at the CALLEE's entry**, beside the limit
  checks, in the same checked entry and at the same state 0: one place,
  every caller covered (direct, indirect, `dyn`, `await`), and D-252's
  bypass is the only elision at a call. Checking at the call site would be a
  second mechanism for one fact.
- **L-2 — The entry check is a predicate function** (`<sym>.req`), the
  `Rules` shape: the checked entry has only `%a` registers and no slots, and
  a contract expression is arbitrary (a `raw f(…)` call, a field read), so it
  is lowered ONCE as a function over the parameters and called from both
  entries. The predicate is not walked for rows (its clauses are the
  function's SITE_DECL row's business).
- **L-3 — A `pure never fails` call is an uninterpreted function**,
  everywhere (contracts and bodies alike): D-242's purity is exactly the
  soundness condition (equal inputs, equal outputs, no effect), and a UF per
  known symbol is what lets a contract call and a body call name the same
  value. An indirect callee has no symbol and stays opaque (V-9/V-14's
  refusal is the load-bearing piece).
- **L-4 — A callee's `ensures` is a hypothesis where continuing means
  success** (`raw`, `?!`, `relay`, a UF application), never at `?|` or a
  `.value` read behind `is_error`; sound because the seam guards or proves
  it. This is what makes contracts a PROOF SYSTEM rather than checks: a
  caller's own `ensures` discharges through its callees'.
- **L-5 — A guard inside a contract expression is the enclosing function's
  site**: a division in a `requires` is a `div-zero` row of the function
  (keyed at the expression), lowered inside the predicate; the same as a
  `Rules` clause's (1.5.2 L-1).
- **L-6 — Conformance is reported, never refused**: an impl whose `requires`
  is stronger than its trait's is `open`, `none`; the impl's own entry check
  traps a caller-through-the-trait's argument at runtime, so the program
  stays safe and the verdict stays a verdict (C-14: nothing in the artefact
  follows the solver's mood).
- **L-7 — One `requires` row per call site and one per function entry**;
  one `ensures` row per return point; the loop's rows per L-8. Rows are
  keyed where checks are, so elision is one lookup and the belts count.
- **L-8 — A loop's invariant is two rows** (entry at the loop statement,
  preservation at the body block) plus one per `continue`; the head check
  elides only when all are discharged. One row with a conjoined goal would
  hide which half failed in the model.
- **L-9 — No path conditions**: the encoder stays path-insensitive. After a
  loop the invariant is a hypothesis (when no `break`), the condition's
  negation is not; after an `if` with a `pass` in one branch nothing is
  assumed. 1.5.4 owns path conditions and `$`.
- **L-10 — `old` names parameters only** (D-243 read literally): a local has
  no value at the function's entry; the emitter snapshots at the body's
  start where no local exists yet, and the encoder's entry symbols are the
  parameters'.
- **L-11 — No `llvm.assume` for contracts**: the elision is the guard's
  removal. A `requires`'s range clauses at the body's start would be a free
  fact, and 1.6's analyzers read `llvm.assume`; the rule that decides which
  clauses qualify (a comparison with a literal) exists for `Rules`
  (`rule_range_i1`). Decided OUT for this subcycle and recorded so it is not
  forgotten: it is an optimization channel, not a language property, and
  every contract test here asserts the traps, not the facts.
- **L-12 — `ensures` runs before the unwind**, with the value in register:
  parameters are still bound, `old` slots live, and the trap route needs no
  unwind (D-014). The `return Result{…}` long form checks only a success.
- **L-13 — The accounting is one trap per clause at an entry or a head, one
  per return point**, so both belts count clauses for `requires`/`invariant`
  and rows for `ensures`/`failsafe-post`, and `rows.txt` names sites so the
  bypass belt counts sites. A belt that counted rows against clauses would
  be the second fact-that-is-not-a-fact of this cycle.
- **L-14 — `failsafe`'s postcondition is the compiler's `ensures`**: code
  −4113, the seam shape at `exit`, REACH-004 for a literal, the row kind
  `failsafe-post`; nothing new in the D-141 space.

## 4. Questions for the user (the plan proceeds under the recommendations)

- **S-43 — `failsafe-post` gets a runtime guard.** D-218's catalogue ratified
  `failsafe-post` with guard `no`; D-014 §3.3 says a `failsafe` returning 0
  is a contradiction. **Recommendation: guard YES** — at every `exit` in
  `failsafe`, `<code> > 0` or trap −4113, which the trap route's re-entry
  rule turns into exit 70, uncatchable: a failed program never reports
  success to its operator, whatever path computed the code. Cost: one
  compare per `exit` in one function. The alternative (`no`) leaves a
  computed `exit 0` in `failsafe` reported as an open row and shipped.
- **S-44 — a `requires` row at an `await` and at a `dyn` call, `retained`;
  D-252 amended to record `limit-subsume` at an `await` the same way.**
  D-252 recorded no `limit-subsume` row at a coroutine callee's call
  ("evidence of a narrowing no build can use"). A `requires` row there
  changes no build either, but it is the only place the program's
  correctness at that call is ever PROVEN; without it a program's `dyn` and
  `async` calls are never verified against their callees' preconditions,
  only trapped. **Recommendation: record them**, word `retained`, and amend
  D-252 so `limit-subsume` at an `await` is recorded the same way — one rule
  for the two kinds. The alternative keeps D-252's text and records
  `requires` rows only at direct sync calls.
