# 1.5.8b — The guarded kinds' rows, and the fields that make their facts true: `sealed` and `hidden`, the constants, `overflow`, the wrapping family, `bounds`, `cast-range`, field limits

**PLANNED 2026-09-19 by `nitpick-compiler_s11` on `35ad9e1` (1.5.8's close), measured first with a throw-away
prototype and four read-only code maps. The user settled SEVEN questions, each the day it was asked, all
first-hand to this seat. Their answers are recorded at step 0 as D-308…D-314 (§3 quotes each).** The last three
were not on the agenda when planning opened. D-311 was a conflict the library workbench (`nitpick-libs_s4`) found
by measuring D-310's reach. D-313 and D-314 answer three memory-safety holes that planning found while working
out what D-308's facts rest on. They come before any row: the facts a verifier trusts must first be true.

## 0. What 1.5.8b is

D-210 §4 promised that "1.5 proves the traps away": overflow obligations join the D-218 catalogue, and a
discharged obligation elides its guard through `llvm.assume`, never `nsw`/`nuw` (D-218.9). VERIFICATION_REFERENCE
§7b assigns this subcycle three kinds with a guard to elide: `overflow`, `bounds`, and `cast-range` (D-306's guard,
1.5.8 step 1). Planning measured before it wrote, and found four things:

- **Half the work is done by facts the encoder already has.** A prototype that writes an `overflow` row at every
  plain-integer `+ - *` of the compiler's own source discharges 1,158 of 2,343 rows. It moves no other verdict.
- **The other half needs facts the language could not state.** To the solver a field read is opaque, and D-220's
  `limit` binds no field. D-308 lets a field carry `limit<Rules>`, and D-309 decides what happens to the rows
  nothing proves: they stay guarded, and are measured and reported.
- **The facts a length rests on were not true.** Any program could write a string's `.len` (DEF-72), the prelude
  `List`'s `cap` (DEF-73), or any element through `List`'s raw `items` pointer (DEF-74). All three are confirmed
  by probes, as out-of-bounds reads and heap corruption. D-313 (`sealed`) and D-314 (`hidden`, and checked `l[i]`)
  close them first.
- **Two smaller defects sit in the constants.** A negated literal is emitted as a checked subtraction that can
  never fire (DEF-70). The constant folder wraps where the run time traps (DEF-71). D-310 refuses a certain
  constant overflow, and D-311 settles how `uint64`'s upper half is written without one. The user also raised the
  wrapping family `+% -% *%` himself (D-312), for Nikola's hot paths, before verification closes.

## 1. Where it starts (measured 2026-09-19 on `35ad9e1`; the refreshed snapshot `b7585e71…` IS the compiler's own emission)

  `@.str` constants, and a count that includes them is off by those strings. The census:

  - `OutOfBounds` (−4099): 15 texts, some of them strings;
  - `CastRange` (−4117): 1.

  Every other kind already has rows: `DivByZero`, `DivOverflow`, `DeadlineExceeded`, `EnsuresViolated`,

  compound `+ - *`, with the goal `smt_range_axiom(result, width, signed)`, a hypothesis after the site as
  `shift-range`'s is. An operand the encoder cannot express records the row `unencoded`, because an opaque result

  - **2,343 `overflow` rows: 1,158 discharged, 1,176 open, 9 budget, 0 unencoded.**
  - Every other kind was unmoved (275 discharged, 138 open, 22 unencoded, 6 checker), exactly the committed

  - Negation has no row. Its guard is emitted with site 0 (`ir_expr.npk:1747`, although the unary node's id is

- **DEF-71 (the folder).** `fold_const` (`type_resolve.npk:858`) computes in one `int64` `num` whatever the type:

- **DEF-72 (the headers).** `.ptr`/`.len`/`.cap` of `string`, `cstring`, slices and `buffer` are typed as places

- **DEF-73 (the List's fields).** `List<T>` is `{ wild T->:items; int64:count; int64:cap; }` (`prelude.npk:2087`),

- **DEF-74 (the List's elements).** Every element access in the tree is `l.items[i]`, an unchecked `wild T->`

## 2. The design

### 2.1 `sealed` and `hidden` (step 1; D-313, D-314; DEF-72)

- **Two keywords, two field qualifiers.** `sealed` means readable everywhere and written only by the declaring
  module. `hidden` means neither read nor written outside it. They enter LEXICAL_REFERENCE's keyword table (the
  generated region), and `gen_tables.py` regenerates the token tables. Both are measured as identifiers across
  `src/`, `lib/`, `npkg/`, `tools/`, `tests/` and the library trees before they land, and a collision is renamed in
  the same commit (the reserved-word table in CLAUDE.md gains the pair). `p_qualifier_bit` (`parser.npk:271`)
  gains `QUAL_SEALED` and `QUAL_HIDDEN`. On anything but a field declaration they are refused where the other
  field-only qualifiers are.
- **"The declaring module"** is the module whose item window holds the struct declaration. For a generic
  instance it is the template's module (K-4). For an inline module it is that module.
- **The write forms** (D-313 §2): an assignment through any path, a compound assignment, a struct literal, a move
  or `pass` out of the field, `@`, `$$m`, and a `Self->` receiver call. A `$$i` claim reads. **The read forms**
  (D-314 §1): a load, a copy out, a `$$i` claim, and a struct pattern binding the field. Outside the module a
  write to a sealed field is `NITPICK-TYPE-079`, and any touch of a hidden field is `NITPICK-TYPE-080`, reported
  at the field access's span. The checker's precedent is TYPE-071's `place_fixed`/`struct_field_fixed`
  (`type_stmt.npk:1143-1209`), which reads a field's qualifier bit and follows a pointer base. The same walk
  answers "sealed/hidden, and whose".
- **The compiler-known containers' headers are sealed by definition.** No declaration exists to carry the bit, so
  the member typer (`type_members.npk:445-477`) marks `ptr`/`len`/`cap` of `string`, `cstring`, a slice and
  `buffer` as non-writable places. Every write form above is TYPE-079 in every module, the prelude included,
  because no module declares them. The prelude's own writes to them are measured at step 1. If any exists, it
  becomes a floor or emitter primitive, never an exemption.
- **Tests.**
  - `tests/types/rejection/sealed_fields.npk`: every write form outside the module; a `$$i` claim accepted; the
    declaring module's own writes accepted.
  - `hidden_fields.npk`: every read and write form.
  - `header_writes.npk`: each built-in kind's three headers under each write form.
  - A program whose module keeps a two-field invariant through sealed fields and functions that maintain it.
  - `lenwrite.npk` (DEF-72's probe) becomes a rejection case.

### 2.2 `List` (step 1b; D-313 §5, D-314 §2-4; DEF-73, DEF-74)

- **`l[i]`.** The checker types an index on a `List<T>` like a slice's: the element's type as a place, readable,
  writable and claimable. The emitter computes the element address from `items` inside its own lowering and
  guards it with the slice's guard, `idx <u count`, `OutOfBounds`, with the same site convention (step 5 adds the
  site parameter and the row). The encoder gives the element read an opaque term, as a slice element has, and the
  index a site.
- **The prelude.** The fields become `hidden wild T->:items; sealed int64:count; sealed int64:cap;`. New checked
  functions sit beside `list_init`/`list_reserve`/`list_push`:
  - `list_pop`: moves the last element out; an empty list traps `OutOfBounds`;
  - `list_truncate(l, n)`: drops elements `n…count−1`; `n` outside `[0, count]` traps `OutOfBounds`;
  - `list_clear`;
  - `list_insert(l, i, v)` for `i` in `[0, count]`;
  - `list_remove(l, i)`: order-preserving, returns the element;
  - `list_swap_remove(l, i)`.

  Each drops or moves owning elements as D-264 requires. Which functions `src/` needs is MEASURED by classifying
  every direct `.count` write first. A pattern none of them serves is recorded and raised, never forced.
- **The sweep.** Every `.items[i]` outside the prelude becomes `l[i]`, and every direct `.count` write becomes the
  operation it performs. The sites are `src/` 665, `npkg/` 1,011 and `tests/` 22. The compiler's own emission of
  itself changes, so the step measures it: the guard census grows by one bounds guard per element access the
  optimizer does not prove, and the step reports the compiler's build time before and after.
- **The bridging refresh** (`bootstrap/seed/README.md`, the 1.5.1b step 5b variant). The builder's embedded prelude
  has neither `l[i]` nor the new functions.
  - **Hop 1, the bridge, never committed:** the tree with `List` indexing, the new prelude functions, and the
    fields NOT yet qualified, with `src/`, `npkg/` and `tests/` unswept. The committed snapshot builds it.
  - **Hop 2, the commit:** the fields sealed and hidden, and the sweep applied. The bridge builds it.

  Then stage 2 == stage 3, installed with a STAMP naming the commit's own tree.
- **Tests.**
  - `listcap.npk` and `listidx.npk` (the DEF-73/74 probes) become rejection cases (TYPE-079/080).
  - `list_index_oob.npk`: `l[l.count]` traps `OutOfBounds`.
  - `list_ops.npk`: each operation, including an owning `List<string>` whose truncate, remove and pop are measured
    by `NPK_HEAP_STATS`.

### 2.3 The constants (step 2; D-310, D-311; DEF-70, DEF-71)

- **The folder computes at the operation's width and signedness** (K-7). `ConstVal` keeps its 64-bit `num`, read
  through the type: an unsigned 64-bit value above 2^63−1 is held as its bit pattern and compared as unsigned.
  - For widths up to 64, `+ - *` and negation are computed exactly: in 64-bit arithmetic with an overflow check,
    or in a 128-bit intermediate where the 64-bit form cannot hold an operand pair. The result is range-checked
    against the declared width. An out-of-range value is `NITPICK-TYPE-076` at the operation.
  - For widths above 64, the folder folds only when both operands and the exact result fit the 64-bit window.
    Otherwise it declines (`cv_none`), and the run-time guard stays: safe, never a wrong value.
  - The folder never traps. Its arithmetic is written so that its own overflow is impossible: every step is
    guarded before it is taken.
- **TYPE-076's reach is the folder's reach:** literals, negated literals, `fixed` constants and `comptime` values.
  A run-time operand is never folded. The code is defined beside TYPE-070 in `type_codes.npk`. It is reported by
  the checker at every binary `+ - *` and unary `-` whose operands fold (`type_expr.npk`), and at `fixed`
  initialisers through `is_const_initialiser`.
- **The emitter folds** (DEF-70). A unary `-` over an integer literal emits the negated constant. A `+ - *` whose
  operands the resolver's folder evaluates, and whose value fits (the checker has refused the rest), emits the
  value. Both skip the intrinsic, the guard and the row. The fold asks `fold_const` at the node, never `.ref`'s
  text.
- **`~0u64`** (D-311). The folder's `~` already folds (`type_resolve.npk:1006`). What changes is the example text:
  LEXICAL_REFERENCE and D-148 carry dated notes already, as of step 0. `derive_gen.npk:201-207` is removed.
- **Tests.**
  - `tests/types/rejection/constant_overflow.npk`: `127i8 + 1i8`, `0u64 - 1u64` in a `fixed` initialiser,
    `-(1i32 << 31i32)`-shaped folds, a `comptime` product, an `int32` sum past 2^31−1, and a wide width that
    declines and is accepted.
  - `constant_fold.npk`: `-1i32`, `2i64 * 8i64`, and `~0u64` beside `(1u64 << 63u64) | 04BF29CE484222325hexu64`,
    each with an exact expected value, and an IR assertion (the `ll_is` pattern) that no `with.overflow` call
    remains for them.
- **The measurement.** The compiler's guard census drops by the constant pairs, 85 by the reconciliation.

### 2.4 The `overflow` rows (step 3; D-309; DEF-75)

- **One row per `IntOverflow` guard**, at the guard's own site key (K-9):
  - binary: `SITE_EXPR` and the node id, as the division rows are;
  - compound: the target's id;
  - negation: the unary node's own id, where the emitter's 0 is corrected to that id;
  - a `simd` integer lane: ONE row over the lanes' conjunction (D-282's shape), because the emitter's guard is one
    any-lane trap;
  - `.sum()`: ONE row at the site with traps N−1 (`record_at`'s traps field), because the scalar core guards each
    fold step at that one site.

  The goal is `smt_range_axiom(result, width, signed)`. It is a hypothesis after the site, and inside a
  proposition it belongs to the proposition (DEF-33). An inexpressible operand makes the row `unencoded`.
- **Elision (D-218.9).** A discharged row's branch becomes `call void @llvm.assume(i1 %no)` over the intrinsic's
  negated overflow bit. The intrinsic stays, and `llctx_elided(ctx, SITE_EXPR, site, OK_OVERFLOW)` is asked
  exactly where the division pair asks. The same applies to the vector guard (`ir_expr.npk:5479`, "No obligation
  row yet").
- **Both runners' tables.**
  - `TRAP_OF_KIND["overflow"] = "-4110"` in both runners.
  - `ASSUME_KINDS` gains `overflow` and `cast-range`: the second is the gap §1 found, and `bounds` joins at step
    5. npkg's list is inline at `verify.npk:628`.
  - `check_obligation_kinds_agree` gains the rule the three kinds lacked: every kind `ok_has_guard` calls guarded
    has a trap code in `TRAP_OF_KIND`. An npkg twin of the check is written.
  - DEF-75: npkg's `read_rows` fails a malformed line by name, as the harness does.
  - `smt_table.npk:8`'s header comment names the eleventh field (tier).
- **The manifests.** `nitpick.obligations` is re-recorded in this step's commit (D-040) with about 2,250 new rows.
  - The 83 verify programs gain their own rows' `expect-obligation` lines. Each count is read from the new
    compiler's `rows.txt` and checked by reading the program, never copied blind.
  - The runner self-checks' three programs gain theirs.
  - New verify tests: a discharged row whose guard is gone from the elided build; an open one whose guard stays;
    the lane form; the `.sum()` form; negation; compound.
- **D-309's report.**
  - The residue by function, open and budget, with the shapes, is written into this plan's record.
  - The compiler's speed is measured three ways: `build/npkc` (plain), the verified compiler (elided), and both
    compiling `src/npkc.npk`. Each is taken under `NPK_HEAP_STATS` beside wall-clock, with instruction counts from
    `valgrind --tool=callgrind` on one compile. The measurement is recorded, never gated (K-13).

### 2.5 The wrapping family (step 4; D-312)

- **Tokens and grammar.** `+%`, `-%` and `*%`, with `+%=`, `-%=` and `*%=`, enter LEXICAL_REFERENCE's operator
  production (the generated region, then `gen_tables.py`). OP_REFERENCE gains the rows. The binary operators sit
  at `+`/`-`/`*` precedence and the compound forms beside `+=`. No existing program changes meaning: no such token
  exists today, and `%` never starts an expression.
- **The checker.** The operands must be the same plain integer type (`intN`/`uintN`), or `simd` integer lanes. On
  any other kind the operator is `NITPICK-TYPE-078`, which names the kind and says why: `tbb` would launder ERR;
  `tfp`, the ternary kinds, `frac`, `dim256` and `complex` have their own arithmetic; floats are IEEE. The
  compound form's target follows the same rule.
- **The emitter.** A plain LLVM `add`/`sub`/`mul` with no flags, or the vector form for lanes. No intrinsic, no
  guard, no row. REACH arms nothing for them (`reach.npk:316-326`). The folder folds them with the wrap and never
  reports TYPE-076. The encoder models the result exactly:
  - unsigned: `(mod (op a b) 2^N)`;
  - signed: two's complement, `(- (mod (+ (op a b) 2^(N−1)) 2^N) 2^(N−1))`;
  - lane by lane for `simd`.
- **Tests.**
  - `tests/backend/programs/wrap_ops.npk`: every operator at `int8`, `uint64` and `int128`, including wrapping
    across MIN/MAX, with python-computed expected values.
  - `wrap_fnv1a.npk`: FNV-1a 64 over three strings against their published digests, with the offset basis written
    per D-311.
  - `wrap_splitmix64.npk`: the first five outputs from seed 0 against the reference sequence.
  - `tests/types/rejection/wrap_kinds.npk`: TYPE-078 on each refused kind.
  - A verify test in which a `*%` site has no row and the value after it is known to the solver.
- **The prelude's FNV step** (`fnv_mix`, `prelude.npk:459`) uses `uint128` widening today. It adopts `*%` if the
  measurement shows the IR smaller and no row regresses. FNV's modular multiply is the stated intent, so the
  operator states the truth, which D-309 allows (K-15).

### 2.6 The `bounds` and `cast-range` rows (step 5)

- **`emit_bounds_guard` gains a site parameter** (`ir_expr.npk:1806`). Every caller has the index expression's id
  in scope: `.trit`/`.nit` 1969, slice 9611, array 9645, `simd` lane 9666, and step 1b's `List` index. The guard
  asks `llctx_elided(..., OK_BOUNDS)`.
- **The encoder records `bounds` rows** with the goal `(and (<= 0 i) (< i len))` over the sign-extended index:
  - for a fixed array, `len` is its type's constant (DEF-22);
  - for a slice, it is the slice's `.len` term;
  - for a `List`, it is `count`'s read term (step 6's fact makes it bounded).

  The range slice's combined guard (`ir_expr.npk:10339-10354`) gets one row, `(and (<= lo hi) (<= hi len))`. The
  inclusive form's `hi + 1` is not a separate row: a wrap there fails the unsigned compare and traps, and the
  row's term is the exact `hi + 1`.
- **`cast-range` rows** at `emit_cast_range_guard`'s two callers (`ir_expr.npk:4660`, `5662`). The goal is
  D-306's condition in the float theory: not NaN, and `LO < f < HI` in `double`, with a `flt32` operand widened
  exactly. A `simd` cast is one row over the lanes. The guard's elision is already written.
- **Both runners:** `TRAP_OF_KIND` gains `bounds` → −4099 and `cast-range` → −4117, and `ASSUME_KINDS` gains
  `bounds`. The manifests are re-recorded. The verify suite gains the new rows' lines, and new verify tests cover
  a proven index, an unproven one, a proven cast and an unproven cast.

### 2.7 Field limits, the ceiling, and the lengths (step 6; D-308; DEF-76)

- **Grammar.** `p_parse_field` (`parse_decl.npk:1029`) accepts `limit<R>` after the qualifiers, as a local does
  (`parse_stmt.npk:642`), into the field's unused slot b (`DeclFieldDecl`). The macro expander's `splice_field`
  (`expand.npk:1787-1828`) clones it, where today it drops b. A `field_limit` accessor is added.
- **Resolver.** `resolve_limit` runs for each field, at module scope. RESOLVE-002 and RESOLVE-011 apply as for
  every limit site.
- **Checker.**
  - TYPE-059 (the subject is the field's type by identity).
  - TYPE-063 extended: `place_limited` (`type_stmt.npk:1094`) answers true for a place whose own field is
    limited, not only a limited root, so a limited field has no address.
  - **TYPE-077 (new):** the rule is folded over the field's vacant value at the declaration (D-225; the vacant

## 5. Decisions taken at planning (each with its reason)

- **K-1 — live memory-safety holes first** (steps 1 and 1b before any row). A fact about a length is worthless
  while a program can write the length, and the holes are exploitable today.
- **K-2 — `List` is its own step (1b).** It needs a bridging refresh and a ~1,700-site sweep. The mechanism lands
  first (step 1), tested on user types and the built-in headers, so the sweep's step changes one thing.
- **K-3 — the built-in headers are sealed by the member typer**, not by a declaration. No module declares them,
  so no module's code may write them, the prelude included.
- **K-4 — a generic instance's declaring module is its template's.** An instance is the template at a type, not
  a new declaration.
- **K-5 — `$$i` of a sealed field is a read; `@`, `$$m` and a `Self->` receiver are writes.** It follows the
  capability each hands out, D-286's reading of the three.
- **K-6 — a struct literal is a write.** It sets every field. A struct with a sealed or hidden field is built by
  its module's functions, which is where its invariant is kept.
- **K-7 — the folder computes at the declared width and never traps.** Its answer is the language's answer at
  compile time, so it must be D-210's. A width it cannot represent declines to fold rather than guess.
- **K-8 — TYPE-076 reaches exactly what the folder folds.** A run-time value is the guard's business.
- **K-9 — the overflow row's site key is the guard's own.** The belts pair rows with traps by (symbol, code,
  group), so a row keyed anywhere else is a red belt. Negation's site 0 is corrected rather than matched.
- **K-10 — elision keeps the intrinsic and assumes its negated bit (D-218.9).** `nsw` would license deleting the
  guard's reason; the assume states it.
- **K-11 — `cast-range` joins `ASSUME_KINDS` at step 3**, with `overflow`. It already elides into an assume, and
  its absence was a gap in both belts.
- **K-12 — the kinds-agree check gains "every guarded kind has a trap code" in both runners.** The three kinds'
  absence from `TRAP_OF_KIND` was invisible to every instrument.
- **K-13 — D-309's speed measurement is recorded, never a gate.** A wall-clock is never a verdict, and the
  instruction counts are the stable half.
- **K-14 — the wrapping tokens are generated from LEXICAL_REFERENCE's region**, like every token, because a
  token list with two homes drifts.
- **K-15 — `fnv_mix` adopts `*%` on measurement.** FNV is modular by definition, so the operator states the truth
  (D-309 allows a bound or spelling that is true).
- **K-16 — `oom.npk` lowers its own `RLIMIT_AS`.** Under a 2^47 ceiling a large request is a bad request, and
  `mmap` with overcommit may grant anything below it. The address-space limit makes the refusal deterministic.
- **K-17 — a limited field's read is a fact wherever it is read.** Every write is checked or proven, the field
  has no address, and a sealed field has no writer outside its module. The read is therefore true by
  construction. A `wild` write through a raw pointer is the author's opt-out.
- **K-18 — the length primitives trap `OutOfBounds`, not a new identity.** A length beyond the address space is
  a view out of bounds. `OutOfBounds` is armed in nearly every program already, and a new universal identity
  would add to the library workbench's re-pin sweep (145 `failsafe`s at pin 3d15ac9 name neither of step 0's
  two).
