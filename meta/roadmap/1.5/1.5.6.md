  disposition column carries the class DEFAULT on every row — "the

  one external (`npk_failsafe`, in `npk_trap`), one indirect call

  `zeroinitializer` (2); 15 globals (`@npk_chtab`/`_len`, `@npk_lgtab`/
  `_len`, `@npk_hsec`, `@npk_hs_*` (5), `@npk_frozen`, `@npk_in_failsafe`,

- **The atomic class.** 46 atomic operations in the floor: 5 `cmpxchg`
  (`npk_mx_lock`'s claim, `npk_clone_exec`'s slot claim `acq_rel`/`monotonic`,
  `npk_step`'s marker consume 1→0, `npk_sarena_bump`'s chunk push, one in
  the driver path), 3 `atomicrmw xchg` (the mutex's contended claim and
  release, `npk_sl_push`'s deadline exchange), 2 `atomicrmw add` (the
  shared arena's `top` and `cap` reservations), 1 `fence`, 17 atomic loads
  and 18 atomic stores. The cross-thread words: a frame's `wake_at` (slot 8:
  every access atomic, `seq_cst`), an executor's `park_word` (slot 5,
  `seq_cst`), `evfd` (slot 12: `release` at creation, `acquire` by rousers),
  the channel table pointer and count (`@npk_ch_tab`/`@npk_ch_n`:
  `release`/`acquire`), the driver registry's state words (`release`/
  `acquire`), the shared arena's `chunk_head`/`top`/`cap` (`seq_cst`). The
  rest of `%npk.exec` (queues, sleepers, `park_at`/`park_pending`,
  `cur_task`, `epfd`, the chain) is the owner thread's alone; `%npk.chan`'s
  fields are read and written under the channel's futex mutex; a frame's
  `chan_next` under the channel lock, `qnext` by its owner, `owner` (slot
  11) written where the frame is born (`ir_func.npk` ~871) before it is
  published under a lock. The scratchpad's `floor_shared.py` lists every
  access of the three shared struct types per function with its ordering.
- **Five plain accesses that race in LLVM's model** (found by reading the
  map above against the threads that reach each word; LangRef: a
  non-atomic load that races with a store returns `undef` — the value
  D-218 (10) banned from the EMITTED IR, which the floor's own text can
  mint at run time; s5 confirmed no audit of this class was ever recorded):
  1. **DEF-44** — `hdr.windup` (slot 2) is stored plain by
     `npk_windup_all` on the JOINER's thread (a wound task may sleep on
     another thread's executor — the function's own comment says so and
     rouses that executor) and loaded plain by `npk_step` at every resume
     (`%wu = load i32, ptr %wup`, line ~2260) BEFORE the `seq_cst` compare-
     exchange on `wake_at` that would order it; the ordering the comment
     claims ("stored before the due stamp and read after the sweep's
     seq_cst load") holds only for a task the sweep found SLEEPING; a ready
     or running task reads the word with no acquire between.
  2. **DEF-45** — `chan.gen` (slot 6) is loaded plain by `npk_ch_get`
     OUTSIDE the channel lock (the early stale check) while
     `npk_ch_reclaim` and `npk_ch_open` write it plain UNDER the lock: a
     handle resolved on one thread against a reclaim on another is a racing
     read; its `undef` feeds a compare whose both outcomes are safe today
     (the lock's re-check follows), which is why nothing ever failed.
  3. **DEF-46** — `@npk_frozen` is stored plain by `npk_trap` on the
     trapping thread and loaded plain by every executor's `npk_step` and by
     `npk_frozen_get`: the word D-063 rests on ("after a trap nothing is
     resumed, anywhere") is read racily by exactly the threads it is meant
     to stop.
  4. **DEF-47** — `@npk_in_failsafe` is a plain load-then-store in
     `npk_trap` (`%in = load; … store i32 1`): TWO THREADS TRAPPING AT ONCE
     both read 0, both store 1, both kill the drivers, both run `failsafe`
     concurrently; and a thread that traps WHILE another's `failsafe` is
     running reads 1, takes the re-entry arm and `exit_group(70)`s the
     process mid-safing. D-063's "other threads stop before `failsafe` gets
     control" is not implemented: `@npk_frozen` stops the next RESUME on
     every executor, and nothing stops a task that is running when the
     trap happens — it runs to its next suspension point, or to its own
     trap. This is the one finding that is a design, not a promotion (§2.9,
     S-69).
  5. **DEF-48** — `npk_thread_join` reads the child's `CHILD_CLEARTID` word
     with a plain `load i32` in its wait loop; the kernel writes it. A
     futex word is read atomically everywhere else in the floor (glibc's
     `lll_wait_tid` reads it atomically for the same reason); today no
     optimiser runs over `npkrt.ll` and the trampoline's asm clobbers
     memory, so nothing hoists the load — the finding is the model's, and
     the fix is one word.
  The trap route also has an ALLOCATION hazard the specification exercise
  makes visible (§2.9, S-70): a `failsafe` body that allocates while
  another thread holds the heap mutex — a thread that trapped inside the
  allocator, or (after S-69) a thread stopped mid-allocation — blocks
  forever on `@npk_heap_mx`, with no deadline: a hang, the uncontrolled
  stop the language exists to make impossible. `npk_hs_report`'s comment
  already names the holder case ("a thread that trapped inside the
  allocator still holds it"). The compiler's own `failsafe` and `npkg`'s
  only `exit`; a user's may build a diagnostic string, which is exactly what
  a `failsafe` should be free to do.

## 2. The design (what S-63…S-70 ratify; written to the recommendations)

### 2.1 Vocabulary

- A **symbol** is one `define` of `runtime/npkrt.ll`; its **class** is
  the classifier's (`asm`/`atomic`/`syscall`/`pure`), unchanged and still
  generated. Its **disposition** is what 1.5.6 adds: one of `trusted` (the
  volatile bottom — `asm`), `specified` (a contract in the spec file whose
  rows are decided), `modelled` (a member of a protocol model), `boundary`
  (a syscall-class symbol whose promise is stated at the kernel boundary,
  with or without decided rows), and `residue` (a symbol, or a clause of
  one, that the profile does not decide — listed by name in TCB.md §5 with
  the reason). A symbol may carry more than one word (`specified` and
  `boundary`: the fd quartet; `modelled` and `specified`: `npk_mx_lock`'s
  totality).
- A **spec** is one `(symbol @name …)` section of `runtime/npkrt.spec`
  (§2.5): contracts over the symbol's parameters, its result, the memory
  before and after, its trap outcome, and its loops' invariants or
  unwinding bounds. A **row** is one obligation the writer derives from a
  spec clause, decided by z3 and recorded in the floor's manifest; a row's
  **kind** is `floor-spec` or `floor-model`.
- A **model** is one `(model name …)` file of `runtime/models/` (§2.7): a
  transition system over a small state whose atomic steps are the IR's
  atomic operations and syscalls, unrolled to a depth with a preemption
  bound; a **bad** predicate names a defect class of the r6 taxonomy; a
  **control** is a variant of the model with one step removed or reordered
  that MUST be refuted (`sat`) — the instrument's own negative control.
- The **translator** is the Nitpick code in `npkg/` (§2.4) that reads the
  floor's text and writes the obligation directory both runners already
  decide; `tools/floorspec.npk` is its thin entry for the harness.

### 2.2 Where the floor's evidence lives (S-63)

- `runtime/npkrt.spec` — the specifications (contracts, invariants,
  bounds, boundary promises, the shared-state classification of §2.9), in
  SMT-LIB2 syntax, beside the floor whose text they describe; hand-written,
  reviewed with the IR (D-203's "reviewed IR"), and held to the IR by the
  belts of §2.11 (a spec naming a symbol or a parameter the floor lacks,
  or a symbol the spec file neither specifies nor excuses, is a red run).
- `runtime/models/*.model` — one protocol per file (§2.7).
- `runtime/npkrt.obligations` — the floor's manifest, in the
  `nitpick.obligations v1` FORMAT (the magic line names the format, the
  file's name says whose rows), written only by `npkg verify --record`, held
  by both runners exactly as the compiler's is (a verdict that moves is a
  red run, D-040); its rows carry the two floor kinds with `tier` the
  encoder's word (`int`, `bv`, `fp`) and elision `none` (nothing in the
  floor elides — the verified build changes no floor byte).
- Decided by the SAME z3 under the SAME profile by the SAME deciders: the
  translator writes `build/verify/floor/` in the compiler's directory shape
  and `z3_verdicts`/`z3_decide` decide it without a change; the kinds
  `floor-spec` and `floor-model` join `smt_kinds.npk`, `Z3_KINDS`,
  `GUARDLESS_KINDS` and the catalogue's marked table with "rows from: the
  floor writer (1.5.6)", so the one-vocabulary instrument keeps holding
  (the compiler never produces them; a `nitpick.obligations` row of a floor
  kind is refused by name in both runners, and a `npkrt.obligations` row of a
  program kind likewise).
- **Why beside the floor and not in `nitpick.obligations`**: a different
  producer (the translator, never the compiler), a different lifetime
  (moves when the floor or a spec moves, never when `src/` does), and the
  runners' belts (`row_function_held`, the trap counts by group,
  `elided_ir_checks`) assume a row names a function the compiler EMITTED —
  floor rows would need an exemption in every belt. The alternative was
  costed and declined.

### 2.3 The theory (S-64)

The floor's rows are encoded under D-218 (4)'s partition exactly as the
program's are, and §1's numbers are the reason: unbounded `Int` with the
exact range axiom of every width (`smt_range_axiom`, 8..128 bits here),
the Int forms of D-279 for a bit operation with a numeral operand, the
bit-vector crossing at ≤ 64 bits (D-280's `BV_CROSS_MAX_BITS`) for a bit
operation with two symbolic operands, floats as tier-1 IEEE terms with the
tier-2 Real-interval twin under D-281's three conditions (the two `fmod`
bodies are the only float code), the twisted kinds absent (the floor has
none). Four idioms the floor uses that the program encoder never meets get
their own SOUND Int forms in the translator, each stated in its header
comment with the fact that makes it sound:

1. `or (shl x 1) y` with `y` a term known in `{0,1}` (the low-bit insert of
   the division loop and the bitmap code): `2x + y` — the shifted term is
   even.
2. `and x (2^k − 1)` at any width: `(mod x 2^k)` (D-279 already); `and x
   (−2^k)` (the align-down `and %ip, -65536`, `and %r15, -16`): `x − (mod
   x 2^k)`.
3. `lshr x k` / `shl x k` by a NUMERAL at any width: `(div x 2^k)` /
   `(mod (* x 2^k) 2^w)` (D-279); by a SYMBOLIC amount at ≤ 64 bits: the
   crossing (D-280); at 128 bits: opaque (a fresh symbol), and the row that
   depends on it is residue by construction — `npk_udivmod128`'s
   `lshr a, %iw` is the one such site.
4. `ptrtoint`/`inttoptr`: identities — an address IS an Int in `[0, 2^64)`
   (the floor's own view; a `ptr` parameter carries the range axiom).

Memory is a byte-addressed UNINTERPRETED FUNCTION `(mem Int) Int` with the
range axiom `0 ≤ mem(a) < 256` on every application, a `store iN` an
explicit chain `mem'(x) = ite(x = a, byte0, ite(x = a+1, byte1, …, mem(x)))`
written as a fresh function definition per store (little-endian, N/8
bytes), a `load iN` the reassembly `Σ mem(a+j)·256^j` (linear, the Int
forms' `+`/`*`-by-numeral), a `load ptr` the same at 8 bytes. No array
sort, no quantifier, no `Seq` (D-218 (6), measured in §1). An `alloca` is a
fresh address symbol with the range axiom and `n` bytes of fresh memory; a
global is a fixed symbolic address per name (distinct from every other
global and every parameter by an explicit `distinct` over the names the
function touches), its initial contents the initialiser for a `constant`
(the class tables) and opaque for a `global`.

### 2.4 The translator (`npkg/floor_ir.npk`, `npkg/floor_smt.npk`)

- **The reader** parses exactly the vocabulary §1 measured and refuses
  everything else BY NAME (`floor: @sym: instruction form not read:
  <line>`), so a floor edit that steps outside the subset is a red run
  naming the line, never a silently unencoded symbol. It reads `define`
  headers (parameters with their IR types, `internal`, `noreturn`),
  blocks by label, the instruction forms listed in §1, the three named
  struct types (`%npk.tls`, `%npk.hdr`, `%npk.exec`, `%npk.chan` — the
  field offsets computed from the x86_64 layout: `i32` 4-aligned, `ptr`/
  `i64` 8-aligned, `i128` 16-aligned, struct alignment its largest
  field's), array types, `getelementptr` over those, constants (decimal,
  negative, hex float, `null`, `zeroinitializer`, `poison` refused by name
  — the floor mints none), the sizeof idiom `ptrtoint (ptr getelementptr
  (T, ptr null, i32 1) to i64)` as the computed constant, the intrinsics
  named in §1, `call` of another floor define, of `npk_sys6`, of
  `npk_failsafe`, and the one indirect call.
- **Straight-line code** becomes SSA-named terms in dominance order: each
  instruction one `define-fun` over the block's path condition — the
  compiler's own scheme (`smt_encode.npk`: a branch condition a hypothesis
  in its arm, an `ite` merge at a join) applied to a CFG rather than an
  AST. A `phi` at a join is the `ite` over its incoming paths' conditions;
  `select` is `ite`; `icmp` and `fcmp` the predicates; an integer op the
  Int form with the width's wrap made explicit where the IR wraps (`add`/
  `sub`/`mul` are `(mod … 2^w)` — the floor's IR has no `nsw`/`nuw`, and
  the allocator's address arithmetic relies on wrapping in exactly one
  idiom, `add %ip, -16`, which the `mod` form handles); `udiv`/`urem` the
  Int `div`/`mod` guarded by the divisor's non-zero-ness as a HYPOTHESIS
  only where the IR's own path proves it (the decimal writer divides by
  10, the validation divides by a class stride read from a constant
  table); `sdiv`/`srem` of `i64` the emitter's truncated forms (`npk_sdiv`
  is not the floor's — the translator writes the `ite` over signs itself,
  the shape `smt_encode.npk` writes for a program's `/`).
- **Calls** to another floor define are INLINED (the call graph has no
  cycle; the belt of §2.11 asserts it), except a call to a symbol the spec
  marks `(summary)` — then the callee's `ensures` is assumed over fresh
  result symbols and its `frame` applied to memory (the sign wrappers over
  `npk_udivmod128`; `npk_arena_free` over `npk_arena_at`). A call to
  `npk_exec` is the per-thread constant `exec` (an address symbol with the
  range axiom). A call to a TRAP function (`npk_trap`, `npk_raise`,
  `npk_heap_bad`/`_oom`/`_badreq`, `npk_exit`) ends the path with the
  outcome `trapped(code)`: the translator carries a boolean `trap` and an
  Int `trap_code` per path, so a spec can state `(ensures-trap …)` — the
  condition under which the symbol traps — and `(ensures …)` holds on the
  non-trapping paths only (the compiler's rule that a proposition holds
  where evaluation does not trap, DEF-33, read the other way round). A call
  to `npk_sys6` is an application of the uninterpreted function `sys` to
  the number and six arguments — its value opaque, its MEMORY EFFECT the
  kernel-effect table's (§2.8): the bytes a syscall may write are fresh
  after the call, every other byte unchanged. `npk_failsafe`'s call and the
  indirect call of `npk_wildx_call` make the path opaque (fresh memory, a
  fresh result) — those two symbols are `residue` by construction, named
  in TCB.md §5 as D-035's and D-014's boundaries.
- **Loops (S-65).** A back-edge is cut at its header. The spec says which
  of two treatments the loop gets, and the row's site text says which was
  used:
  - `(invariant LABEL I)`: the loop-carried `phi`s and the memory at the
    header become fresh symbols constrained by `I`; three rows result —
    `init` (the entry edge establishes `I`), `preserve` (`I` and the loop
    condition and one body execution re-establish `I`), and every
    `ensures` clause after the loop is decided under `I ∧ ¬cond`. `I` is
    quantifier-free; a universal it needs ("every byte below `i` is
    copied") is written over the section's declared free symbols
    (`(free j Int)`), and the writer asserts the hypothesis at THOSE
    symbols and demands the conclusion at the SAME symbols — the
    single-instance rule §1 measured (`memcpy`'s preservation at 2,138).
    The rule is sound (a proof of `I'(j)` from `I(j)` for arbitrary `j` is a
    proof of `∀j. I'(j)` from `∀j. I(j)` when the step needs `I` at no other
    point) and incomplete (a step that needs `I` at another point is
    `open`, and the spec author adds `(inst e)` — an extra instantiation of
    the hypothesis at `e`, never of the conclusion). The header comment of
    `floor_smt.npk` states this rule and its two halves.
  - `(unroll LABEL N)`: the loop is unwound `N` times and the condition
    after the `N`-th body is asserted FALSE as a hypothesis; the row's site
    reads `[≤N]` and the disposition says `bounded`. Sound for every
    execution of at most `N` iterations; a loop whose trip count is a
    numeral (`npk_udivmod128`'s 128, `npk_hs_put_dec`'s ≤ 20 digits, the
    registry walks' 16, the class tables' 14) unwinds EXACTLY and the row is
    not bounded — the writer knows a numeral bound when the condition
    compares the counter against a numeral and the counter starts at one.
  - A loop with neither annotation is a refusal by name (`floor: @sym:
    loop LABEL has no invariant and no bound`), never a silent opaque.
- **What the translator does not do, by decision**: no ghost variables (a
  fact the IR does not compute is stated as residue, not invented — the
  division identity's `2^i` is the case); no quantifiers in any file it
  writes; no per-row budget; no interpretation of inline asm or of the
  three `asm` symbols; no reading of `module asm`; no `switch`, no
  `invoke`, no exceptions; no vector or aggregate arithmetic beyond
  `extractvalue`/`insertvalue` of the struct returns the floor uses.

### 2.5 The spec file (`runtime/npkrt.spec`)

One S-expression per symbol, SMT-LIB2 syntax throughout, comments `;`.
Names: a parameter by its IR name without `%` (`a`, `b`, `dst`, `n`);
`result` (an aggregate's members `result.0`, `result.1`); `mem` and `mem2`
(memory before and after — `'` is not an SMT-LIB symbol character);
`trap` and `trap_code`; `exec`; a global by its IR name without `@`
(`npk_hsec`) as its ADDRESS, its contents read through `mem`; `(free j
Int)` declares a free symbol for the section; `(load64 mem addr)`,
`(load32 …)`, `(load8 …)` are the writer's reassembly macros, `(store64
mem addr v)` the store macro. Clauses:

- `(requires P)` — a hypothesis over the parameters and `mem` (a precondition
  the floor's callers keep; the translator does NOT check callers — the
  floor's callers are the emitter and the floor itself, and the emitter's
  call discipline is the program encoder's business under D-201's table).
  A `requires` is recorded in the row's site text so a reader sees the
  assumption.
- `(ensures P)` — one row per top-level conjunct, on the non-trapping paths.
- `(ensures-trap P)` — one row: `trap ⇔ P` (the trapping paths and the
  code); a symbol without it that can trap gets the default row `¬trap`
  under its `requires`, i.e. the spec claims it never traps.
- `(frame (lo len) …)` — one row: for the free symbol `x`, `x` outside every
  listed range ⇒ `mem2(x) = mem(x)`. A symbol with no `frame` clause claims
  `mem2 = mem` pointwise (a pure function of its arguments) — the default,
  and a row.
- `(loop LABEL (invariant I) | (unroll N) | (inst e)…)` — §2.4.
- `(summary)` — the symbol is inlined nowhere; callers assume its clauses.
- `(residue "…")` — the symbol has no rows, or a clause is not decidable,
  and the sentence says why; it is copied into TCB.md §5 verbatim by the
  generator. A symbol with neither rows nor a residue sentence fails the
  belt.
- `(boundary "…")` — the syscall-class promise, prose, copied into TCB.md's
  disposition column (§2.8).
- `(shared …)` — the shared-state classification (§2.9), one section for
  the file.

Example, the shape every executor must reproduce for a new symbol:

```
(symbol @memcpy
  (free j Int) (free x Int)
  (requires (or (<= (+ dst n) src) (<= (+ src n) dst)))
  (loop head (invariant (and (<= 0 i) (<= i n)
                             (=> (and (<= 0 j) (< j i)) (= (load8 mem2 (+ dst j)) (load8 mem (+ src j))))
                             (=> (or (< x dst) (>= x (+ dst i))) (= (load8 mem2 x) (load8 mem x))))))
  (ensures (=> (and (<= 0 j) (< j n)) (= (load8 mem2 (+ dst j)) (load8 mem (+ src j)))))
  (ensures (= result dst))
  (frame (dst n)))
```

### 2.6 The rows and the verdicts

- A row per clause as §2.5 says; its `kind` `floor-spec`; its `symbol` the
  floor symbol (`@memcpy`); its `hash` the SHA-256 of the row's canonical
  SMT text plus symbol plus kind (D-218 (8), `lib/nhash.npk`); its `site`
  field `spec:<clause>:<ordinal>` or `spec:<clause>:<label>` (`spec:
  invariant-preserve:head`) with `[≤N]` appended for a bounded loop; its
  `role` `conform` (no guard — the word the runners already know for a
  guard-less row); `group` and `traps` `0`; `encoded` `1`; `tier` the
  writer's word. A model's rows (§2.7) carry `kind` `floor-model`, `symbol`
  `model:<name>`, `site` `bad:<predicate>:K<depth>:D<preempt>`.
- Verdicts: `discharged` is a proof; `budget` is RESIDUE (the row and its
  reason appear in TCB.md §5, generated); **`open` in the floor is a run
  failure by name** — `floor: @sym: <clause> is refuted (a counterexample
  exists; either the floor does not meet its specification or the
  specification is wrong; `npkg verify --explain` writes the model)` —
  because nothing in the floor is a guard to retain: a `sat` is a defect or
  a misstatement, both stop-the-line. `npkg verify --record` refuses to
  write an `open` floor row, and both runners refuse a committed one.
- `--explain` covers the floor's rows exactly as the compiler's: a model
  per open row (only ever seen on the way to fixing one), a reason per
  budget row, a core per discharged one.

### 2.7 The models (S-66)

A model file is a transition system the writer UNROLLS to SMT-LIB2 in
QF_LIA (the probe's shape, §1): state variables with sorts, an initial
predicate, per-thread named steps (guard, update, the IR blocks the step
abstracts), kernel steps (a spurious return, a delivered signal), bad
predicates, a depth `K` and a preemption bound `D` (BPOR's idea in the
symbolic setting: the unrolling asserts that the thread index changes at
most `D` times over `K` steps — a bug needing more context switches than
`D` is outside the bound and the row's site says `K` and `D`), and
controls. One row per bad predicate; one query per control, which must be
`sat`. The models, each in its own file, with the bad predicates named
from the r6 taxonomy:

Correspondence: every step names the IR blocks (`(ir @npk_step nothing
idle2)`) it abstracts; the belt `check_floor_models_current` (harness) and
`floor_models_current` (npkg) parse the floor, take every atomic
instruction and every `npk_sys6` call in the modelled symbols, and fail
when one sits in a block no step of any model names — the model can be
wrong about a step's meaning, but it cannot be silent about a step. The
soundness of SC over the atomics is stated in the file header of
`floor_model.npk` and rests on §2.9's table: every cross-thread datum in
the modelled protocols travels through a `seq_cst` operation or through a
`release`/`acquire` pair whose payload is read only after the acquire, and
x86-TSO plus LLVM's ordering rules make an SC interleaving of those
operations the only observable order. Liveness (a due task is eventually
run, a walker's spin ends) needs fairness the bounded unrolling cannot
state and is residue by name; 1.5.7's schedule-exploration harness is the
instrument for the real code, and every model here is the shape its
mocked primitives will be driven through.

### 2.8 The syscall boundary

- **The generated table.** TCB.md gains a second marked region,
  `<!-- BEGIN floor-syscalls -->`: one row per symbol that issues or
  reaches a syscall — its direct syscall numbers with their names and the
  transitive set — produced by the same walk `_floor_classes` makes
  (`floor_syscalls.py`'s, promoted into `harness.py` as `_floor_syscalls`
  and into `npkg/floor.npk`), held by `check_tcb_syscalls_current`. The
  region's head lists the 24 numbers with their names once.
- **The kernel-effect table** (in `floor_smt.npk`, one row per number the
  floor uses; a number outside it is a refusal by name): what the
  translator assumes a syscall does to memory and to the result — `read`
  writes `[buf, buf+result)` when `result ≥ 0` and nothing otherwise;
  `write`, `close`, `futex`, `epoll_ctl`, `epoll_pwait` (writes the events
  array up to `result` entries), `clock_gettime` (writes the 16-byte
  timespec), `getrandom` (writes `[buf, buf+result)`), `openat`, `mmap`
  (returns an address; the mapping's bytes fresh and zero for anonymous
  private — the floor's only mmap shape), `munmap`, `mprotect`,
  `sched_getaffinity` (writes the mask), `pidfd_send_signal`, `exit`/
  `exit_group` (the path ends), `eventfd2`/`epoll_create1`/`dup3`/`prctl`/
  `arch_prctl`/`getppid` (no memory effect), `clone`/`execve` (never
  reached by a translated symbol — `asm`). The table IS "what the kernel
  promises" (TCB.md §3's kernel row, made concrete) and a reader accepts it.
- **The envelope symbols get rows.** With `sys` uninterpreted and the table
  applied, the fd quartet (`npk_open`/`_close`/`_read`/`_write`), the file
  helpers (`npk_path_exists`, `npk_write_file`, `npk_read_file`,
  `npk_read_stdin`, `npk_to_cstring`), the clock (`npk_mono_now`), the
  string builders (`npk_string_concat`, `npk_string_slice`,
  `npk_int_to_string`) and the descriptor drop (`npk_ofd_close`) are
  SPECIFIED: the D-141/D-069 envelope discipline — a negative kernel result
  becomes the error field with a ZEROED value slot, a non-negative one the
  value with error 0; a short write is returned, not retried; `read`'s
  count never exceeds `cap`; `mono_now` is seconds·10⁹ + nanoseconds of
  the timespec the kernel wrote; a builder's result has `len` ≤ `cap` and
  the bytes the spec names. Their allocator calls are `(summary)` calls of
  `npk_alloc_internal`/`npk_alloc_impl` (a fresh 16-aligned address with
  `n` fresh bytes, or the trap outcome), so the string builders are decided
  without the allocator being.
- **The rest of the class** — the allocator proper (`npk_alloc_impl`,
  `npk_small_alloc`, `npk_chunk_new`, `npk_large_new`, `npk_ralloc`,
  `npk_dalloc`, `npk_aalloc`, `npk_calloc`, `npk_buffer_new`,
  `npk_wild_release_all`, `npk_heap_init`, `npk_hmap`/`_hunmap`,
  `npk_chtab_insert`, `npk_lg_insert`), the arenas' allocation paths, the
  frame arena's, the executable pages, the waits (`npk_*_wait`,
  `npk_thread_join`, `npk_run_until`), the thread start/entry/exit, the
  process start, the reactor's `npk_io_unwatch`, and the trap route's
  `npk_exit`/`npk_hs_report` — carry a `(boundary "…")` promise each and
  NO rows in this subcycle, with the reason: the allocator's properties
  (D-150's header/payload disjointness, validate-before-dereference over
  the whole heap) are the class of invariant D-233 assigned to leg A and
  named SeaHorn's expected tenant; the waits are `modelled` through their
  primitives; the thread and process boundaries are the kernel's. The
  promise sentences are the specification a reader gets today; the rows
  are 1.6's or the depth tool's, and TCB.md's residue list says so per
  symbol.

### 2.9 The shared-state rule, the trap route's whole-program stop, and the trap route's allocator (S-67, S-69, S-70; DEF-44…DEF-48)

- **The rule (S-67).** Every load or store of a word two threads can
  reach is either an ATOMIC access (`monotonic` at least — LangRef: an
  atomic load never returns `undef`) or a PLAIN access that a named
  happens-before edge orders, and `runtime/npkrt.spec`'s `(shared …)`
  section says which, per word: `(word %npk.hdr 2 atomic)`, `(word
  %npk.chan 3 lock chan.lock)`, `(word %npk.exec 0 owner-only)`, `(word
  %npk.hdr 11 born-before-publish)`, `(word @npk_hsec once-before-threads)`
  … The belt `check_floor_shared_current` (harness) and
  `floor_shared_current` (npkg) list every access of the three shared
  struct types and of every `global` (not `constant`) in the floor, and
  fail when an access exists whose word the section does not classify, or
  when a word classified `atomic` has a plain access. The classification's
  truth is the reviewer's (a `lock` word's accesses must sit inside the
  lock's critical section — the belt checks the DOMINATOR: every access of
  a `lock`-classified word in a function that takes the lock is dominated
  by the lock call and post-dominated by the unlock, or the function is
  named as `(under-lock @fn)` by its caller's discipline); the
  `owner-only` and `born-before-publish` classes are stated with the
  edge that orders them and are what the reader must accept (TCB.md §5).
- **The four promotions (step 0; DEF-44, DEF-45, DEF-46, DEF-48)**:
  `npk_windup_all`'s `store i32 1, ptr %wp` → `store atomic i32 1, ptr %wp
  release, align 4` and `npk_step`'s `%wu = load i32, ptr %wup` → `load
  atomic i32, ptr %wup acquire, align 4`; `npk_ch_get`'s generation load
  and `npk_ch_reclaim`'s and `npk_ch_open`'s generation stores →
  `monotonic` (the lock orders the stores; the early read outside it needs
  only to be a defined value); `@npk_frozen`'s store in `npk_trap` and its
  loads in `npk_step` and `npk_frozen_get` → `seq_cst`; `npk_thread_join`'s
  `%t = load i32, ptr %tp` → `load atomic i32, ptr %tp monotonic, align 4`.
  No behaviour changes on x86 (each is the same instruction with an
  ordering the hardware already gives); the model changes. `npkrt.o`'s
  digest moves; the step's notice names both digests and the reason.
- **The whole-program stop (S-69; DEF-47).** D-063 says a trap is a
  whole-program event and that other threads stop BEFORE `failsafe` gets
  control; the floor stops resumes and nothing else. The design:
  1. **A thread registry** — `@npk_thread_reg`, 64 entries × 2 words
     `[ state i32 | tls ptr ]` in `.bss` (the driver registry's shape:
     preallocated, CAS-claimed, walked by the trap route, fixed capacity
     with `npk_thread_start` refusing when full by the trap it already
     raises for a failed clone, `−4103`); `npk_thread_start` claims a slot
     (`cmpxchg 0 → 1 acq_rel`), writes the TLS pointer, publishes
     (`store atomic 2 release`) BEFORE the clone; `npk_thread_join`
     retires it (`store atomic 0 release`) after the tid word cleared; the
     main thread's slot is claimed at `npk_tls_boot`. The registry replaces
     no existing word; the join list is unchanged.
  2. **The stop signal** — at `npk_start`, `rt_sigaction(SIGUSR1 = 10,
     &act, NULL, 8)` over the kernel's own 32-byte `{ handler, flags,
     restorer, mask }` laid out by hand: `handler = @npk_stop_handler`,
     `flags = SA_RESTORER (0x04000000)`, `restorer = npk_sigreturn`,
     `mask = 0`. SA_RESTORER is not optional on x86_64: the kernel's
     `x64_setup_rt_frame` refuses to deliver a signal whose action has no
     restorer (the process would die of SIGSEGV instead of stopping), so
     the floor gains a two-instruction `module asm` stub beside
     `npk_clone_raw` (`npk_sigreturn: movl $15, %eax; syscall` —
     `rt_sigreturn`, never executed because the handler never returns).
     No `SA_SIGINFO`, no `SA_NODEFER`. The handler is `define void
     @npk_stop_handler(i32)`: `atomicrmw add @npk_stopped, 1 seq_cst`,
     then `futex_wait(@npk_stop_word, 0)` in a loop forever
     (`@npk_stop_word` is never written; a spurious return re-waits). It
     allocates nothing and touches no runtime state but the counter. The
     process's pid is recorded once at `npk_start` (`getpid`, 39) into
     `@npk_pid`.
  3. **The arbitration** — `npk_trap` replaces the load-then-store of
     `@npk_in_failsafe` with `cmpxchg @npk_in_failsafe, 0, <self tls>
     seq_cst seq_cst` where `<self tls>` is `%fs:0` (the TLS block's
     self pointer, `npk_exec`'s neighbour — a second one-instruction asm
     read, `npk_tls_self`, classified `asm`): the winner proceeds; a loser
     whose recorded holder IS itself (re-entry: `failsafe` trapped on the
     trapping thread) takes the hard exit 70 as today; any other loser
     PARKS (the handler's loop, called directly) — it never runs a second
     `failsafe`, never exits the process, and dies with the winner's
     `exit_group`.
  4. **The stop** — after `@npk_frozen` is set and BEFORE
     `npk_driver_kill_all`, the winner walks the registry and sends
     `tgkill(@npk_pid, tid, SIGUSR1)` (234) to every published slot but its
     own (the tid from the slot's TLS word, which `PARENT_SETTID` wrote),
     then waits for `@npk_stopped` to reach the count it signalled, under
     the join deadline read from its executor (`npk_join_deadline`) — a
     thread that does not stop in time (a state the kernel does not
     interrupt) is proceeded past, and the notice in TCB.md §5 says so; the
     wait is a `futex_wait` on the counter with the deadline, not a spin.
     Then the drivers, then `failsafe`, on the trapping thread, with every
     other thread parked in its handler or exited.
  5. **`npk_exit` from a non-holder** while a holder exists (a program
     `exit` racing a trap on another thread): parks likewise — the
     controlled exit is the failsafe's, and D-224 (`exit` is process exit)
     keeps its meaning because the process still exits, by the winner.
  The `trap-route` model (§2.7) is built FIRST on today's floor — its bad
  predicates are `sat` there, the negative control that proves the model
  sees DEF-47 — then on the new one, `unsat` at the recorded depth. Two
  syscalls join the floor's table (`rt_sigaction` 13, `tgkill` 234,
  `getpid` 39 — three), the notice names the reason, and CONCURRENCY
  §5.3/§10's D-063 paragraph gains the mechanism.
- **The trap route's allocator (S-70).** D-014 and the heap's own header
  say "the trap path allocates nothing" and "`failsafe` runs on
  preallocated state"; today `failsafe` allocates from the same heap the
  program was using, under the same mutex — and after S-69 a stopped thread
  may hold that mutex forever. The design: a **failsafe region** —
  `@npk_fs_region`, 1 MiB of `.bss` (a size stated once in the floor;
  untouched pages cost nothing), a bump pointer `@npk_fs_bump`; once
  `@npk_in_failsafe` holds a winner, `npk_alloc_impl` (the one entry every
  allocation reaches) bumps from the region instead of taking the heap
  mutex — 16-aligned, zeroed by construction (`.bss`), never freed;
  `npk_dalloc`/`npk_ralloc` on a region pointer are a no-op (the process
  exits with the region) and `npk_dalloc` on a HEAP pointer during
  `failsafe` is a no-op too (freeing into a heap another thread may have
  left torn is the hazard, and a leak at the exit that follows costs
  nothing; D-151's leak check does not run on the trap path — `npk_exit`'s
  registry walks stay as they are); region exhaustion is a trap inside
  `failsafe`, which is the re-entry rule's hard exit 70 — the same answer
  as today's `HeapOom` inside `failsafe`, and TCB.md §5 states the
  region's size as the bound a `failsafe` body lives within. The
  alternative — a checker rule refusing every allocation reachable from
  `failsafe` — is declined: it refuses diagnostics (the one thing a
  `failsafe` most needs to build) and forbids the library tier's
  `failsafe` idioms, where the region keeps every body legal and makes the
  claim the documents already make true.

## 3. Questions for the user (OPEN_DECISIONS §2e S-63…S-70; §2f DEF-44…DEF-48)

**S-63 — where the floor's evidence lives (§2.2)**: `runtime/npkrt.spec`,
`runtime/models/*.model` and `runtime/npkrt.obligations` beside the floor;
the manifest in the `nitpick.obligations v1` format, written only by `npkg
verify --record`, held by both runners; the two kinds `floor-spec` and
`floor-model` in the one catalogue; `open` a run failure in the floor.
**Recommendation: ratify as written.** Alternative costed: floor rows in
`nitpick.obligations` — a second producer in one file and an exemption in
every belt that assumes an emitted function.

**S-64 — the theory (§2.3)**: the floor's rows under D-218 (4)'s partition
exactly as programs' — Int with range axioms, D-279's Int forms plus the
four floor idioms named, the ≤ 64-bit crossing, floats in two tiers —
with memory a byte-addressed uninterpreted function and explicit store
chains, no array sort. **Recommendation: ratify; the alternatives are
measured out** (QF_BV: the division identity `unknown` at 16 bits; QF_ABV:
`memcpy` at 16 bytes needs 20× the budget; the Int partition decides both
shapes in milliseconds).

**S-65 — loops and the residue (§2.4)**: an invariant when the spec supplies
one (quantifier-free, universals instantiated at the goal's free symbols,
the rule stated with its incompleteness), unwinding to a stated `N`
otherwise with `[≤N]` in the row; no ghost variables, no per-row budget.
The named residue: `npk_udivmod128`'s multiplicative identity (a 128-bit
multiplier equivalence — measured undecidable under any budget tried; its
remainder bound, total `b = 0` answer, termination and the four sign
wrappers relative to the core ARE decided), `fmod`/`fmodf`'s loop (the
special-value cases, `|r| < |b|`, the sign and the exactness of each
subtraction step are decided; the loop's exit value against a real
`fmod` is not), `npk_wild_live_count`'s two-table sum (bounded to the
tables' current sizes is meaningless; the count's relation to the bitmap
is the allocator's invariant — leg A's). **Recommendation: ratify.**
Alternative costed: a larger budget for floor rows — declined; the 100×
run bought nothing for the multiplier and the array case was an encoding.

**S-66 — the models (§2.7)**: six bounded transition systems in SMT-LIB2
through the pinned z3 under the one profile, SC over the atomics with the
shared-state classification as the ordering evidence, depth and preemption
bound recorded per row, negative controls required `sat`, the
correspondence belt over block labels; liveness residue by name; the
r6 taxonomy's classes as the bad predicates. **Recommendation: ratify.**
Alternative costed: TLA+/TLC — a second engine against D-218 (1)'s "one
solver", a second determinism profile, a JVM on the workbench; declined.

**S-67 — the shared-state rule (§2.9)**: every word two threads can reach
is atomic or ordered by a named edge, classified per word in the spec
file, held by a belt in both runners; the four promotions of step 0 are
the rule's first application. **Recommendation: ratify; step 0 runs before
the rest.** The caution s5 raised is built in: a plain access that is by
design (`owner-only`, `born-before-publish`, `once-before-threads`) is
classified and stated in TCB.md §5, never silently promoted.

**S-68 — TCB.md's generated columns (§2.10)**: the disposition column, the
`floor-syscalls` and `floor-residue` regions generated and held; §5
finalized with the seven new acceptances. **Recommendation: ratify.**

**S-69 — the trap route's whole-program stop (§2.9; DEF-47)**: a thread
registry, a stop signal whose handler parks forever, `cmpxchg`
arbitration of the failsafe holder (a re-entering holder exits 70 as
today, any other loser parks), the winner signalling every other thread
and waiting under the join deadline BEFORE the drivers and `failsafe`,
`npk_exit` from a non-holder parking. Three syscalls join the floor
(`rt_sigaction`, `tgkill`, `getpid`). **Recommendation: build it in this
subcycle (step 1) — D-063 promises it, the floor does not deliver it, and
two concurrent traps today run two `failsafe`s or exit the process
mid-safing.** Alternative costed: document the gap as residue — declined;
this is the actuator scenario the language exists for, and a decision to
leave it would be a decision to ship the hazard.

**S-70 — the trap route's allocator (§2.9)**: a 1 MiB `.bss` failsafe
region that `npk_alloc_impl` bumps from once a failsafe holder exists,
frees no-ops during `failsafe`, exhaustion the re-entry exit 70.
**Recommendation: build it (step 2).** Alternative costed: a checker rule
refusing allocation reachable from `failsafe` — declined (it refuses
diagnostics and the library tier's idioms; the region makes "`failsafe`
runs on preallocated state" true instead of forbidding what it is for).
