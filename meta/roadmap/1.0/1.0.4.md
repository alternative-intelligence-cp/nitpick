# 1.0.3 — Traits, impls, and generic families (D-161)

**Cycle 1.0.** Trait-method dispatch on concrete receivers, default methods, and
impls over generic families. Decided: `D-161` (and the default-method half of the
grammar audit).

## What it builds

- **Trait-method dispatch on concrete receivers** — `x.trait_method()` where `x`'s
  type implements the trait, resolved through the impl table (the machinery exists
  for inherent methods; this extends it to trait impls) including **inherited
  defaults** (a trait method with a body, used by an impl that omits it).
- **Family impls** (D-161): the one contained `p_parse_impl` production change —
  after the generics window, a type; a following `:`-type means the first was the
  target (`impl:<T>:Box<T>:Sized2`), else the target is empty (blanket, unchanged).
  Per-instance impls (`impl:Box<int32>:Sized2`) already work and gain regression
  coverage. Overlap between a family and a per-instance impl for one (type, trait)
  is refused (§4.1, no specialization).
- **Derive on a generic subject** synthesizes the family form; until proven, it
  refuses generic subjects by name (never the broken `impl:Box:Eq` it emits today).

## Tests

A trait implemented on a concrete type, dispatched through a method call; a default
method inherited and overridden; `impl:<T>:Box<T>:Trait` checked and an instantiation
dispatching to it; an overlap refused; `#[derive(Eq)]` on `Box<T>` either generating
the family impl or refusing by name.

## Done when

Trait methods dispatch on concrete receivers with defaults; family impls parse,
resolve, check, and lower; overlap refuses; derive-on-generic behaves per D-161; the
fixpoint holds.
