# 1.0.4b — Family impls: the D-161 amendment, and the frontend half

**Cycle 1.0.** The piece 1.0.4 split out after the production as D-161 wrote it
broke the blanket form. Opens with the amendment, which turned out to need no
new rule at all.

## The amendment (settled, and simpler than the draft feared)

D-161 proposed distinguishing the family form from the blanket form by
lookahead, and floated three candidate heuristics. None is needed, because
**D-111 already settled the shape: a blanket impl must name a trait.** So after
a generics window the SEGMENT COUNT decides, with nothing to guess:

| written | segments | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `impl:<T: Printable>:Loggable` | one | blanket — the segment is the TRAIT, the declared parameter is the target (D-105) |
| `impl:<T>:Box<T>:Sized2` | two | family — target, then trait |

A trait-less family (`impl:<T>:Box<T>`) is the one-segment case and is refused
for exactly D-111's reason — it would add methods to every type of a family the
writer may not own. The parser reads `:` then a type per segment; the 1.0.4
attempt parsed a type unconditionally and treated a following `:` as the
discriminator, which consumed the blanket form's trait as a target.

## What landed (frontend: parse, resolve, check, dispatch)

- **The production**, as above. Blanket and concrete forms unchanged.
- **`impl_is_blanket` tests the empty TARGET SLOT**, not the generic count. A
  family impl declares generics too, so the old test made every family impl a
  blanket one — discarding its target and reporting its methods as not matching
  the trait.
- **An impl's own parameters are bound before its target resolves**
  (`impl:<T>:Box<T>` reads `Box<T>` with `T` declared right there) — the same
  move `bind_own_generics` makes for a function's signature.
- **`find_method` matches a family impl by DECLARATION.** `Box<int32>` and the
  impl's `Box<T>` are different interned types that share a declaration, which
  is what identity is (D-090) — one step less general than `blanket_applies`.
- **`bind_blanket` serves both shapes.** A blanket impl's single parameter is
  the receiver itself; a family impl's are the receiver's own type ARGUMENTS,
  taken positionally. (Positional is exact for what the grammar admits —
  `Box<T>` with the declaration's parameters in order. A target like
  `Box<Pair<T, U>>` would need real unification, and that is a decision to
  raise, not to guess.)
- **A family impl's method call is recorded as an instantiation request** in
  the same `FnInstTable` a generic function uses, deduped the same way, now
  carrying the parameter window and the owning impl (a method's declaration
  carries neither, and the emitter has no decl→owner link).

Result: `impl:<T>:Box<T>:Sized2` parses, resolves, type-checks, and a call on
`Box<int32>` dispatches to it — all green under `tools/check.npk`.

## Emitting the specialization — the fix, and the four attempts before it

A family impl's method body would not emit, and the two spellings failed
differently, which is what located the cause:

- `func:width = int64(Box<T>:self)` reported **TYPE-001** on `Box<T>` (`T`
  unbound);
- `func:width = int64(Self:self)` reported **TYPE-005** on `Self` (unbound).

Two spellings, two different unbound names, one root: the specialization was
emitted from the top-level instantiation walk, which sits OUTSIDE the binding
of `Self` and of the impl's parameters. Four attempts each closed a real
sub-part — threading the parameter window, recording the owning impl, pushing
both the `generics` and `enclosing` slots, resolving the impl's target under
the substituted arguments — and none was sufficient, because the context
itself was wrong.

**The fix is placement, not plumbing.** A family impl now emits its own
recorded instantiations from inside `emit_impl_block`, where `Self` and the
impl's parameters are already bound for the non-generic case; the global walk
skips impl-owned entries and handles plain generic functions only. `Self` is
bound to the SUBSTITUTED target (`Box<int32>`, not the template's `Box<T>`),
which is what makes the emitted parameter type match the caller's argument.

Both spellings now run (`family2`/`family3`, exit 13), and traits, defaults,
UFCS and generic functions are unaffected.

## Done when

A family impl's method emits and runs. **Met.**

## What this subcycle did NOT do, and who owns it

Two of D-161's clauses are not met, and both are worse than merely absent —
each has a WRONG behaviour today, measured:

- **Overlap is not refused at the impl.** A family impl and a per-instance impl
  for one (type, trait) both collect; the program is refused only at a CALL, as
  `NITPICK-TYPE-020` "ambiguous method". §4.1 says overlapping implementations
  are a compile error, which should fire at the second impl whether or not
  anyone calls it — a library with an overlap that nothing in it calls compiles
  clean and breaks its consumer.
- **`#[derive]` on a generic subject emits a broken impl.** `#[derive(Eq)]` on
  `struct:Box<T>` synthesizes `impl:Box:Eq` — the bare declaration, no
  arguments — and the reader gets three `NITPICK-TYPE-016` "generic arity"
  errors pointing at `<derived-1>`, source they never wrote. D-161 says
  explicitly: refuse the generic subject by name until the family form can be
  synthesized, because a refusal naming the real declaration is strictly better
  than errors against synthesized text.

**Owner: 1.0.4c**, scheduled immediately after this subcycle rather than left
to be remembered. Both are contained: the overlap check is a pass over the
collected impl table (the shape `check_coherence` already has), and the derive
fix is either the family synthesis D-161 describes or the by-name refusal it
mandates in the interim.
