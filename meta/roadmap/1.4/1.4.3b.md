# 1.4.3b — The consuming `pick` (D-216) — PLANNED (execution-grade)

> `pick (move(v)) { … }`: the selector consumed, ownership transferring
> into the matched arm. Runs AFTER 1.4.3 (the loop-carried move
> analysis) because the moved-from bookkeeping this form writes is the
> machinery 1.4.3 just hardened.

## Semantics (D-216's record, operationalized)

- `move(v)` as the pick selector: legal exactly when `v`'s type owns
  (type_drops true) — on a non-owning selector, `move` here refuses as
  it does anywhere the move is meaningless (match the existing
  move-of-non-owner diagnostic).
- After the pick, `v` is MOVED-FROM on every path (the D-208 analysis
  records it at the pick, not per arm).
- The matched arm's BOUND payloads become owned locals: they drop at
  the arm's scope exit like any owner; binding an owning payload in a
  consuming pick is what TYPE-046 exists to permit here — the copy
  refusal keys off the pick's consuming-ness.
- UNBOUND owning payloads of the matched variant drop AT THE MATCH
  (emitted in the arm's prologue before user code): the value was taken
  apart; nothing may leak.
- The enum's own drop does NOT run for `v` (its drop flag clears at the
  pick — the emitter's existing move-clearing machinery, applied to the
  selector).
- A NON-consuming pick over an owning enum is unchanged: legal while no
  arm binds an owning payload (lending reads), TYPE-046 at any arm that
  does — the message gains a pointer: "…or consume the value:
  `pick (move(v))`".

## The work

1. **Checker** (`type_stmt`/`type_expr` pick paths): detect the
   consuming selector shape (`ExprMoveExpr` operand); thread a
   `consuming` bit through arm checking; TYPE-046's pick-arm refusal
   keys off it; unbound-owning-payload arms get no refusal (the drop is
   emitted); the move analysis records the selector's move (reuse the
   move-site machinery — the selector is an ordinary move expression,
   most of this falls out).
2. **Emitter** (`ir_stmt.npk` pick lowering + `bind_payload`): under a
   consuming selector — bound payloads: the existing extract-and-store
   becomes the OWNING copy (no change to bytes; the difference is drop
   flags: the arm's slot arms a drop flag, the source's flag cleared);
   unbound owning payloads of the matched variant: per-variant prologue
   drops via `drop_fn_sym` at `variant_payload_slot_at` offsets (the
   1.4.1 cores — three consumers become four).
3. **Wildcard and payload-less arms** in a consuming pick: the matched
   variant is whatever the tag says — a wildcard arm must drop the LIVE
   variant's owning payloads without knowing it statically: emit the
   tag-switch drop (call the enum's own `@"npk.drop.<tid>"` on the
   still-intact value in the wildcard arm's prologue — the body exists
   since 1.4.1 and does exactly this dispatch).
4. **Tests**: executed `pick_move.npk` — string payload bound and
   length-checked in the arm (the 1.4.1 probe's blocked read, now
   legal), OwnedFd payload bound and used (fd closes at ARM exit —
   fcntl probe), unbound-payload arm (fd closes AT MATCH), wildcard arm
   over an owning variant (enum drop body does the work), use-after-
   consumed refusal case (MOVE code), move-of-non-owning-selector
   refusal, double-consume-in-loop refusal (D-208 catching the
   composition). Rejection rows join the move suite.
5. **Docs**: TYPE_REFERENCE's pick section + the enum section gain the
   form; AST unchanged (no grammar work — `move(v)` parses today);
   CLAUDE.md status line at commit.
