## 0. What 1.6.1 is, and is not

1.6.0's gate named the engine (D-322: NIKOS wins by row 2; Clam decided out). 1.6.1 makes
leg A a STANDING STAGE of both runners: NIKOS, pinned by commit and digest and read from
`nitpick.toml`, run over every real-backend program's emission on every full run, its
verdicts committed as an alarm ledger where a NEW alarm on an unchanged tree fails the
stage. Getting there is the port list the gate's reading sized (`1.6.0.md`, the step-4
record, row 5), landed in the user's NIKOS tree one measurable item at a time, and one
change to the emission (E-8, D-322 (5)). It is NOT: leg C (1.6.2, Alive2, with E-7 first);
the evidence package or the compiler's own whole-program cadence (1.6.3); any escalation
tool (SeaHorn, SAW/Crux stay named escalations with entry criteria); any language change.

**The rule this plan is written under.** D-319: a transform between the artifact and the
analyzer invalidates the evidence. At the gate NIKOS could not read our emission without
`ikos-pp` (its importer refuses `select` and `switch`, has no atomics, and `-opt=none`
still lowers switches, atomics, constant expressions and selects), so every gate row was
over a transformed module — measured at one and eight sites of difference (step 4's
`-opt=none` reading), small and of covered classes, and still a transform. 1.6.1 removes
it: the importer learns every instruction our emission contains, the driver reads the
emission's own bitcode, and a belt holds the analyzer's input to the emission's text. That
is step 2, before any model, because a model measured through a transform is a model of the
transform's output.

## 2. The design

### 2.1 The pinned-tool table (`nitpick.toml`, `[verify.nikos]`) — D-204's "pinned AND READ"

```
[verify.nikos]
commit      = "<40-hex>"                       # alternative-intelligence-cp/nikos, the commit engines.sh builds
sha256      = ["<ikos-analyzer>", "<ikos-pp>", "<ikos-import>"]   # the three binaries' digests, in this order
options     = ["-a=boa,dbz,nullity,uva,sio,uio,shc,poa,uaf,dfa", "-d=interval", "--proc=inter",
               "-j", "1", "--name-values", "--display-checks=no"]  # every invocation is BUILT from this list
entry-points = "main"                          # the plain form's root; the whole form's roots are the floor's
assume-check = true                            # S-104: the second decider of each z3 discharge (§2.4 (4))

                                               # moving no verdict at 3,800x the time
```

Both runners refuse a table with a missing row BY NAME before anything runs, refuse a
binary whose digest differs from the pinned one (the z3 precedent, D-218 (1); the
toolchain stays a VERSION pin by D-265 — an evidence tool is a digest pin), and refuse an
option list that carries a thread count above one or a wall-clock knob (D-297's and
D-321's rule: a verdict is a function of the input, the tool build and the budget). The
stage never builds NIKOS: `engines.sh build nikos` on the workbench is the one builder, and
the stage's first act is `engines.sh check`.

### 2.2 The input: the emission as it is (D-319)

The stage's input for a program is its PLAIN emission — the `.ll` the harness assembles,
with `target datalayout` (E-8) — assembled by `llvm-as` and handed to `ikos-analyzer`
directly, with no `ikos-pp`. The belt `analyze-input`: `llvm-dis` of the bitcode the
analyzer read equals the emission's text modulo the header lines (the round-trip belt of
1.6.0 step 2, re-homed). What the importer must therefore accept, read at 1.6.0 step 4 and
counted in the emission's census (`1.6.0.md` §1): `select` (path split, ~30–40 lines),
`switch` (~40–60), `atomicrmw`/`cmpxchg`/`fence` (their SEQUENTIAL semantics — NIKOS
analyses one thread, and the floor's cross-thread protocols have their own evidence, 1.5.6's
models and 1.5.7's explorer; the record says so where the import lands), constant
expressions (`lower-cst-expr` in the importer, not the module), and a function with several
exits (`UnifyFunctionExitNodes` is an AR-side normalisation if AR needs it, never an LLVM
one). `ikos-pp` is not run by the stage; it stays a tool of NIKOS's own suite.

The whole-program forms (the floor linked in) are NOT the stage's input: the floor has its
own evidence (1.5.6's spec and rows, 1.5.7's explorer), and TCB.md's tables are the model
the analyzer consumes (§2.4 (2)); the two gate programs' whole forms stay in the gate's
runner as the standing measurement of the executor wall's port (§2.4 (3)).

### 2.3 The alarm ledger (`nitpick.alarms`)

One row per SITE — the notion `gate_alarms.py` reads off `ikos-report`: (program,
function, statement id, check kind, status), with the message SHAPE (numbers and names
normalised), the number of contexts reaching it, the CLASS (`floor-model`,
`overflow-intrinsic`, `root-parameter`, `signedness`, `precision`, `defect`) and the
DISPOSITION (`true-defect` — a program defect, fixed before the row is committed;
`imprecision-accepted` — a fact the domain cannot hold, with the reason;
`fact-missing` — a fact the emission could state, with the lead that states it;
`model-pending` — a floor model item of §2.4 (2), with its step). A row without a
disposition fails the stage. On every full run both runners diff the run's sites against
the ledger: a NEW site fails the stage by name (D-233: a new alarm on an unchanged tree is a
stop sign), a VANISHED site is printed and must be re-recorded in the commit that removed
it (D-040's rule for the obligation manifest, carried over: a verdict that moves is a red
run, never a rebaseline). `--record` writes the ledger from a run, in the commit that
changes what is analysed. The runner self-check plants one of the eight controls' defects
(`plant.py`) and requires the stage to fail by name — the instrument must find what is
planted, every run.

## 5. Decisions taken at planning (each with its reason)

- **Plain forms are the stage's input, whole forms are the wall's measurement.** The floor
  is proven by 1.5.6 and modelled by its entries; analysing it again in every program would
  make every program's ledger carry the floor's 245 sites (the gate's whole forms) and prove
  nothing the floor's rows do not.
- **Transform-free before models** (§0): D-319, and the gate's own measurement that the
  transform moves sites.
- **The overflow model splits on the range, not on inequality**: an interval domain cannot
  refute `w != ext(t)`, so the overflow path would stay feasible forever (C.2 of the record).
- **A flagless `add`/`sub`/`mul` takes its operand's sign and is not checked by
  `sio`/`uio`**: in our emission every flagless one wraps by definition (the wrapping family,
  the floor's arithmetic); the guarded ones are the intrinsics, checked at their model.
- **`dbm` is not the domain**: measured at 1.6.0 step 4 as moving no verdict on the plain
  program at 3,800× the time and not finishing the whole form in fifteen minutes.
- **No `--hardware-addresses`**: a range wide enough for the floor's mappings switches the
  check off (B.2 of the record); the allocator-entry model is the remedy, not a range.

**What landed.** A VIEW is a fourth party kind of D-286's aliasing walk (`PARTY_VIEW`,

body views what it was handed, a view moved through a call or pushed into a `List` — is
