## 0. What 1.6.1d is, and is not

Sixteen findings in five classes. **Memory faults in safe code** — a consuming `pick`'s
binding outside the move rules (DEF-118), a view's root freed through a callee's `move` out
(DEF-119), a write through a `$$i` holder (DEF-123), a `for` binding outliving its loop in
the emitter so a later use of an outer name reads the loop's slot and past it (DEF-127), a by-value
receiver method called on a pointer receiving the pointer's bits as the struct (DEF-134, found at
execution by the step's own accept file).
**Silent wrong answers** — a `for` over a range running zero times at its type's edges
(DEF-128), `loop`/`till` widening an unsigned bound by its sign (DEF-129), `till` counting
down from a negative limit (DEF-130). **Leaks** — `(<-p) = v` never dropping the old
pointee (DEF-120), a consuming `pick`'s binding never dropped (DEF-121), `to_cstring`'s
block never freed (DEF-122). **Over-restrictions and refusals of what the reference
promises** — a `move` parameter re-initialised after a move unreadable (DEF-124), a swap
through a lent `dyn`'s method refused as a stored view (DEF-125), `<=>` refused by the
emitter (DEF-131), a negative range-pattern bound refused by the emitter (DEF-132). **The
diagnostics and the documentation** — a TYPE-007 naming two types by one word (DEF-126),
seven reference sentences the compiler contradicts (DEF-133).

It is NOT: a change to any rule the listener did not find wrong (every fix below restores
what a decision already says, or amends one decision by the user's word — S-109, S-110);
a snapshot refresh (nothing in `src/` needs the new emitter until measured otherwise; step
2's `for` fix changes the compiler's own emission but the builder's loops are the
snapshot's — see §6); a floor byte (S-109's chosen shape may move the floor at step 3, and
says so).

## 1. Where it starts (main at landing 73, `0902e48`; every line number is that tree's)

**The move analysis** (`src/frontend/analysis/bindings.npk`): `symbol_slot` (1772) answers
a slot for a `SYM_STMT` local and a `DeclParamDecl` parameter and −1 for everything else,
so every rule "quietly does nothing on −1" (its own header, 1.4.3's lesson); the resolver
(`resolve.npk` 506–517, `bind_pattern_names`) declares a `pick` pattern's names as
`SYM_PAT` with no `local_ord`; `assign_assign` (1091) returns before marking when the target
is not a `SYM_STMT` symbol (1185: `if (sym.origin_kind != SYM_STMT) { pass NIL; }`), so a
parameter's re-assignment never clears `moved`.

**The escape analysis** (`src/frontend/analysis/escape.npk`): `record_mut_of_place` (1138)

## 2. The design

### 2.1 DEF-118 — a consuming `pick`'s binding enters the move analysis (step 1)

**The defect** (F-003): `pick (move(e)) { (Som(x)) { string:a = move(x); string:b =
move(x); } }` compiles and double-frees (exit 95); `if (…) { string:a = move(x); }
raw obs_s(x)` compiles and reads the 0xAA poison (exit 70). The local twins are MOVE-001.

**The fix.** (1) The resolver hands every pattern binding an ordinal in the function's dense
numbering exactly as it hands one to a local: in `resolve_pattern`'s enum arm and in
`bind_pattern_names`, after `symtab_declare`, `symtab_set_local_ord(r.tab, psym,
r.local_next); r.local_next += 1` (the guard `psym > 0`, as for a local). (2) `symbol_slot`
gains the arm `if (s.origin_kind == SYM_PAT()) { pass symtab_local_ord(x.tab, sym); }` — one
resolver of slots, the fifth kind it was written to prevent a fifth mistake about. (3)
`assign_pick` marks each arm's bindings ASSIGNED in the arm's state (`state_mark(one,
slot)`) before the guard and the body: a binding introduced by the construct is assigned,
and `state_mark` clears `moved`/`freed`, which is what a loop's second iteration needs when
the arm re-binds a name moved in the first (D-208's loop-carried states). The binding
symbols of a pattern are found through one map built at `assign_init` — `pat_first[pattern
id]` / `pat_next[symbol]` over one scan of the symbol table for `SYM_PAT` symbols, the shape
`alias_init` (alias.npk 148) uses for `stmt_sym` — never by scanning the table per arm
(1.5.2d's scaling lesson). (4) Nothing else: `assign_read` already skips the unassigned
check for a non-`SYM_STMT` symbol (1436), reports moved/freed first (1426–1435), and the
`ExprMoveExpr` walk marks through `binding_slot` → `symbol_slot`. A LENDING binding is a view
the typer refuses to move (TYPE-047), so its slot is never marked moved; tracking it costs
nothing and keeps one rule.

**Tests.** `tests/analysis/rejection/pick_binding_moves.npk`: the second move (MOVE-001),
the read after a conditional move (MOVE-001), a read after a move in one arm and a plain read
in another arm of the same name (accepted — arms are exclusive, F-6's repair), a loop whose
arm moves the binding each iteration (accepted), a struct destructure's binding moved twice
(MOVE-001); exactly the set {MOVE-001}, three sites. `tests/backend/programs/pick_binding_
churn.npk` (step 3's DEF-121 shares it): the listener's `ctl_pick_single_move` shape exits 0.

**Risk.** A `for` binding is a `DeclParamDecl` and was already tracked. The ordinal space
grows by the pattern bindings, and `state_new` is sized by `symtab_fn_slots`, recorded after
the body; no other reader of `local_ord` assumes locals and parameters only (measured: the
three readers are bindings.npk 1775/1781 and the resolver's own 358/598).

### 2.2 DEF-119 — a `move` out through a pointer is a mutation of the pointee (step 1)

**The defect** (F-004): `at_fr(@x)` with `func:at_fr = NIL(string->:p) { string:t =
move(<-p); }` while `uint8[]:v = string_bytes(x)` is live compiles and the view reads freed
bytes; `$$i x` to the same callee likewise; `$$m x`, an overwriting callee through `@x`, and
a direct `move(x)` are BORROW-015.

**The fix.** `record_mut_of_place` peels ONE leading dereference before asking the root, as
`record_flow_of_store` does (1094–1096): `if (tn.kind == ExprDerefExpr) { place =
expr_operand(tn); }` — then `move(<-p)` records path 0 (the whole pointee) on parameter `p`'s
mutation chain, the caller's `admit_written(@x)` → `check_written` meets the view party of
`x` and BORROW-015 refuses as the overwriting twin already does. The peel lives in
`record_mut_of_place` so every caller agrees (the D-240-style "one predicate" rule); the
existing peel in `record_flow_of_store` stays and is harmless. The `$$i x` face is DEF-123's
(§2.3 (b)): a shared claim handed to a callee whose summary writes through that position.

**Tests.** `tests/analysis/rejection/view_root_moved_through_callee.npk`: `@x` to a
callee that `move(<-p)`s (BORROW-015), `$$i x` to it (BORROW-013 by §2.3), `@x` to a callee
that moves a FIELD out (`move(p.s)` — already recorded, kept as the control that the peel
changes nothing there), and a callee that only reads through `p` (accepted) — exact sets.

**Risk.** A callee that moves out of every pointer it is handed now writes its summary
so; a caller holding a live view of that root is refused where it read freed memory. The
sweep under both checkers (§4 step 1) measures the tree and the listener's repositories;
a site that appears is a real hazard by construction.

### 2.3 DEF-123 — a write THROUGH a shared holder, and a shared claim handed to a writer (step 1)

**The defect** (F-008): `string->:p = $$i x; (<-p) = nw();` compiles and runs (the root
write `x = nw()` under the claim is BORROW-013, and `$$m`'s holder writes); `store($$i x)`
with `func:store = NIL(string->:p) { (<-p) = nw(); }` compiles. D-286 names both: "a write
through a shared claim's holder" and "a write-capable access under `$$i`".

**The fix**, in alias.npk, two arms of one rule — a write-capable access reaches a SHARED
claim's storage through its holder:
(a) **The dereference.** `acc_place` (and `acc_expr`'s `ExprDerefExpr`, `ExprAddressOfExpr`,
`ExprMoveExpr` and the pointer-receiver case of `acc_call`) asks, for a place whose chain
bottoms in `<-q` with `q` a bare pointer binding, `refuse_shared_holder_write(x, q, at)`:
if a live party has `kind == PARTY_SHARED && holder == q` → BORROW-013 with the message of
786–796 (the same text: "a write-capable access through `q`, which holds … (a SHARED claim:
readers only, D-286) — claim it `$$m` to write through it"). A read through `<-q` stays a
read. The check is the one at 786 factored into a helper taking the holder symbol, called
from the existing root path and from the new deref path — one rule, one text.
(b) **The pass-through.** In `acc_call`, for a CLAIM argument of kind SHARED (the `$$i x`
spelling) and for a PLAIN argument that is a bare pointer binding holding a live SHARED claim
(the `f(p)` spelling; `sym_holds_claim` 1125, narrowed to SHARED), the callee's mutation
summary of that position decides: a known callee (`escape_call_fn > 0`) with any written
path (`escape_mut_first(callee, pos) >= 0`, or −2 "could not read") → BORROW-013 ("a `$$i`
claim is handed to a callee that writes through it (D-286: readers only) — claim it `$$m`,
or hand the callee `@`"); a known callee that writes nothing through it → admitted, as
today; an UNKNOWN non-builtin callee (a `dyn` method, a function value; `escape_call_fn ==
0` and not `call_is_bare_builtin`) → refused, the closed direction every walk here takes;
a bare builtin → admitted (its row is the reference's; measure first: if the tree hands
`$$i` to no builtin that writes, say so in the record, else the row's `Views` column is the
reading). The receiver position (`p.m()` with `p` a pointer holder and a `Self->` method)
is position 0 of the same rule.

**Tests.** `tests/analysis/rejection/shared_holder_write.npk`: the deref store through a
`$$i` holder, `move(<-p)`, `@(<-p)` handed on, a pointer-receiver call on `<-p` (four
BORROW-013), `$$i x` to a writing callee and `f(p)` with `p` a `$$i` holder to a writing
callee (two BORROW-013), the `$$m` twins and a reading callee (accepted) — the exact set.
`tests/accept/shared_holder_reads.npk`: every read through a `$$i` holder, and `$$i` to a
callee whose summary writes nothing.

**Risk.** (b)'s unknown-callee arm may refuse `$$i` handed to a `dyn` method in the tree
or the listener's code; the sweep says how many, and each is a claim the walk could not
check — the closed direction D-286 asks for. If the count is large and every site is a
reader, the record says so and the arm stays (a reader spells `@`, D-286's "spell `@` for an
address that claims nothing").

### 2.4 DEF-127 — a `for` binding ends with its loop in the emitter (step 1)

**The defect** (F-011): `int64:i = 100; for (int64:i in 0i64...3i64) { … }` then `i` reads
3; with an outer `int32[4]:v` and a loop `int32:v`, `v[3]` after the loop reads 12 bytes
past the loop's 4-byte slot (-O0 11, -O2 10); with an outer `string:i`, `llc` refuses the
module. The checker ends the binding's scope at the loop (`ctl_no_outer` is RESOLVE-002);
only the emitter keeps it.

**The fix.** `emit_for` takes `int64:mark = fnem_mark(x.fe)` before dispatching to its arms
and `fnem_release(x.fe, mark)` at every arm's end (the range arm at 1379, the indexed and
iterator arms at their `done_l`), so the binding — sync (`fnem_local`) and coroutine
(`fnem_bind` of the frame slot) alike — stops resolving when the loop ends, as a block's
declarations do (`emit_block` 491/529). The binding carries no flag ("pick-arm and loop
bindings … never drop", 380), so nothing else runs at the release.

**Tests.** `tests/backend/programs/for_binding_scope.npk`: the same-type shape (exit by the
outer value), the array shape (the outer element read after the loop, at -O0 and -O2 alike
by the harness's two legs), the string shape (compiles and runs), a coroutine with the same
three, and a nested `for` whose inner binding shadows the outer loop's; `ctl_block_shadow`'s
shape as the control. The emitter unit tests (`ir_stmt.npk`'s expectations) may move by the
release's absence of text: run `harness.py --only tests/backend/` before the commit.

### 2.4b DEF-134 — a by-value receiver through a pointer is the pointee, loaded (step 1)

**The defect** (found at execution, by `tests/accept/shared_holder_reads.npk`'s `g = raw q.peek()`
exiting 7 where 4 was written): `q.peek()` with `Box->:q` and `peek = int64(Box:self)` is
admitted by the checker as D-098's one-level auto-dereference (the receiver it fits is the
pointee's type, `type_members.npk` 2014–2030, 2475), and the emitter's receiver rule — "the
receiver's own type decides the form: a pointer is passed, anything else by value"
(`ir_expr.npk` 9166–9172) — had the auto-ref dual for a `Self->` method on a value (1.0.9b,
9200–9215) and no load for a `Self` method on a pointer: `ptr %q` was passed where the callee
expects `%Box` by value, and `self.n` read the pointer's low word (3 at -O0, garbage at -O2;
measured on `0902e48`, the same at both compilers).

**The fix.** In `emit_method_call`'s value branch: when the receiver's recorded type is a
pointer and the callee's parameter 0 (substituted) is neither a pointer nor a `dyn`, the
pointee is LOADED (`load <T>, ptr %q`) and that value is what `emit_fit` receives — the dual
of the auto-ref, one level, exactly the checker's reading. A `dyn` parameter or a pointer
parameter keeps the pointer. Nothing is `temp_take`n: the loaded copy is a loan (a plain
by-value parameter, TYPE-085), as `bx.peek()`'s copy is.

**Tests.** `tests/backend/programs/byval_recv_ptr.npk`: through a plain address, the value
receiver itself, a UFCS free function through the pointer, a pointer parameter's `p.peek()`,
the pointer-receiver twin as the control (auto-ref, then the value re-read), a `$$i` holder's
pointee, a generic instance's method through a pointer; every case its own exit, at both
legs.

### 2.5 DEF-128 — a `for` over a range at its type's edges; S-110 (step 2)

**The defect** (F-012): `for (int8:i in 0i8..127i8)` runs zero times; `for (uint8:i in
100u8..200u8)` zero; int16/int32/int64 at their maxima and uint16/uint32/uint64 across the
sign bit likewise. Two causes, both in the design: D-145 normalised every range value
HALF-OPEN at construction (`..` stores `hi + 1`), so `lo..MAX` cannot be represented at its
width at all ("wraps under the +1 and the bounds guard traps it — a loud outcome for a
corner with no honest answer", D-145's own words — true of a SLICE, whose guard fires, false
of a `for`, which has no guard and runs zero times in silence; and the corner has an honest
answer: `0u8..255u8` is the byte loop); and the head compares SIGNED whatever the element.

**The decision this needs — S-110 (§3).** The recommended shape: **a range value KEEPS ITS
SPELLING**: `range<T>` is `{ T:lo; T:hi; bool:inclusive }`, built by the literal from its
own payload (no `+ 1`, no wrap), read by every consumer with the flag. D-145 is amended
(its "no consumer ever asks which spelling built a range" was the blueprint rule applied to
a representation that cannot hold a value the language can spell). The alternatives — a
wider `hi` (every width needs the next; `uint64` has none), a `{lo, count}` pair (a count
of 2^64 has no width), refusing `lo..MAX` (refuses the byte loop) — each fails a value the
language spells today.

**The fix under S-110.** (1) `ll_type` of `TY_RANGE` is `{ T, T, i8 }` (`ir_types.npk` 600,
879; the layout in `type_layout.npk`/`types.npk` 1196 sized accordingly); the literal
(`ir_expr.npk`'s `ExprRangeExpr`) stores `lo`, `hi` and `n.payload` as the flag; `#size_of`
follows. (2) `emit_for`'s range arm: `empty = inclusive ? (lo > hi) : (lo >= hi)` and `last =
inclusive ? hi : hi - 1`, both by the element's signedness (`type_is_signed(btid)`:
`sgt/sge` or `ugt/uge`; `hi - 1` computed only on the non-empty path, where `hi > lo ≥ MIN`
makes it exact), branch to `done_l` on empty; the head keeps its invariant check and enters
the body; the increment tests `cur == last` BEFORE `cur + 1` and leaves on equality — so no
`add` at the top of the type, no compare that can wrap. The encoder's reading of a range
`for` (1.5.4 step 3: start, the in-range symbol, the exit value) is over the AST's bounds and
is unchanged; the `terminate` rows are by construction (TYPE-072). (3) Every other reader of
a range VALUE's second word — measured first by grep for `extractvalue` over `TY_RANGE`
values and for `range<` in the tree (14 spellings): the slice view `l[lo..hi]` reads the
range EXPRESSION's children today (alias.npk 468–478 folds the bounds and the payload; the
emitter's index-range likewise — verified at step 2's first act) and keeps doing so; a
spelled `range<T>` value's uses in the tree are re-read one by one.

**Tests.** `tests/backend/programs/for_range_edges.npk`: the listener's seven shapes
(int8 `125..127` three trips, `0..127` 128, int32 and int64 `max-2..max` three, uint8
`100..200` 101 and `100...200` 100, uint32 across 2^31 ten) and the three controls, each
trip count the exit; `for (uint8:i in 0u8..255u8)` 256; the empty shapes (`5..4`, `5...5`,
`0u8...0u8`); a `range<uint8>` value bound first and iterated. `tests/backend/ir_types.npk`
gains the `range<T>` layout pin.

### 2.6 DEF-129 and DEF-130 — the counted loops widen by their sign and `till` ascends (step 2)

**The defects** (F-013, F-014): `loop(100u8, 200u8, 1u8)` runs 156 times counting DOWN to
−55 (the bounds `sext`ed, `200u8` read as −56, the direction inferred from `100 < −56`);
`till(200u8, 1u8)` 56 times down to −55; a uint32 loop across 2^31 traps `IntOverflow`;
`till(-3i64, 1i64)` runs three times down to −2 where CONTROL_REFERENCE §2.4 and D-022 say
"`till` ascends from 0 … `limit <= 0` | zero iterations".

**The fix**, in `emit_counted`: (1) `loop_i64` takes the operand's signedness
(`type_is_signed(v.type_id)`) and widens with `zext` for an unsigned operand, `sext` for a
signed one — the working width stays `i64` (a `uint64` is the identity extension; its
compares are unsigned, below); (2) the loop records `unsigned` beside its direction, and
every compare of the counter against the limit is `ult/ugt` for an unsigned loop, `slt/sgt`
for a signed one (the direction test at 1157, the head's pair at 1183–1186, and the

### 2.7 DEF-120 — `(<-p) = v` drops the old pointee (step 3)

**The defect** (F-005): 121 grid cells, every owning type; 1,000 stores keep 46,043 B live
at exit; an `OwnedFd` stored over is left open (exit 26). The member and element targets
drop (D-186); the dereference target, the fourth store shape, does not (`emit_assign`
938–958: "a pointer target is somebody else's value and is left alone").

**The rule.** A dereference target `(<-P) = v` drops the old pointee value before the
store when the pointee's type owns (`type_drops_recorded`) AND the pointer expression `P` is
not a `wild` place — the manual regime's storage (`alloc`, `alloc_managed`, `#ptr_add`, a
`wild`-qualified binding, field or cast: `QUAL_WILD` is a QUALIFIER on the declaration or
the cast, never part of the type, parse_type.npk 14–16) is uninitialised or the author's,
and the closed direction for a wrong free is NO free (a leak is the safe failure, a bad free
is the unsafe one). "`P` is a wild place" is decided by one predicate,
`place_is_wild(x, P)`: a bare binding or parameter whose declaration carries `QUAL_WILD`
or `QUAL_WILDX` (`stmt_quals`/`param_quals`), a member path whose root or any field on the
path is so qualified (`field_quals`), an index path over such storage, an `=>! wild …->`
cast (`expr_cast_quals & QUAL_WILD`), a `#ptr_add`/`#wild_slice`/`#wild_ptr` result, a call's
result of a builtin whose row returns `wild` (`alloc`, `alloc_managed`, `ralloc`), and any
`P` that is not a place chain at all (a call, an operator) — everything the emitter cannot
name is wild to this question. The escape analysis has this reading already for its own
purpose (escape.npk 2581–2604, `wild_source`), and the emitter's predicate is written to
give the same answer on the same shapes (a unit test holds the pair).

**Why the drop is sound where it fires.** A non-wild `T->` names a LIVE managed value: a
binding's (every declared binding holds a value or D-225's vacant one, whose drop is a
no-op), a field's or element's (a moved-out one holds the vacant value, S-26), a pointee's
own field (the same), a `move` parameter's — with ONE gap this step closes: `emit_move_out`
of a WHOLE binding (874) cleared the flag and left the stale header in the slot, so
`p = @x; string:t = move(x); (<-p) = v;` — legal today (a held `@` is D-286's plain party,
and `move(x)` is not its conflict) — would free `t`'s body through the new drop. So (2): a
moved-out whole binding keeps the type's canonical VACANT value too — `emit_move_out`'s
identifier case takes the place's address, loads the value, stores `zeroinitializer` and
calls the vacant helper as the field/element case does (the flag clear stays as the fast
path for the scope-exit drop) — one rule for every moved-out place (D-225, S-26), and the
deref drop then meets a vacant value there. `exit`'s path runs no drops (D-183's
amendment) and is untouched.

**Tests.** `tests/cost/deref_store.toml` (a recipe at N and 4N under `NPK_HEAP_STATS`, the

**As built (step 3).** The predicate is not a twin held equal by a test: the escape analysis's

`src/frontend/analysis/wild_places.npk` (`expr_wild_provenance`, `place_wild`,

imports it — ONE reading. Its answer for a shape the whitelist does not name is `false` — for the

## 5. Decisions taken at planning (each with its reason)

- **Four landings, by severity, not one.** Sixteen fixes in one commit would put a
  three-hour harness between every measurement and its cause; the user's rule orders the
  faults first, and a fault's fix should not wait for a leak's.
- **DEF-118 and DEF-121 are one construct in two steps.** The analysis half is a fault
  (step 1) and the emitter half a leak (step 3); they share a test file, extended at step 3.
- **The deref drop is gated on `wild`, not on the pointee's provenance.** The emitter
  cannot know where a `T->` came from; the qualifier is the author's statement of the
  regime, and every producer of manual storage returns or is spelled `wild`.
- **A moved-out whole binding is vacated.** The flag alone was enough while nothing read
  the slot after a move; the deref drop reads it through a held `@`. One rule for every
  moved-out place (S-26) is cheaper than a second rule about held addresses.
- **`till` never infers.** D-022's table says zero iterations at `limit <= 0`; the
  emitter's inference contradicted the decision, and the evaluator copied the emitter.
- **The counted loop's working width stays 64.** D-022's machinery; widening to 128 for
  one family is a second machinery.
- **The range keeps its spelling (recommended, S-110).** The only representation that holds
  every value the language spells.
- **`cstring` string-shaped (recommended, S-109).** One rule for the two string kinds.
- **The shared-holder rule's unknown-callee arm refuses.** The closed direction of every
  walk in the aliasing analysis (its header); a reader spells `@`.
