# 0.6.1 — Expansion, bounded

**Cycle 0.6 — Macros and comptime.**

## Goal

A program's macros expand to a fixed point, or the expansion is refused for
exceeding its bound.

## The rule

**Module-level expansion iterates until no invocation remains.** `bug594` is the
case: `outer!()` expands to `{ inner!(); f3 }`, and `inner!()` expands to
`{ f1; f2 }` on the next iteration. One pass is not enough, and the loop repeats.

**And the loop is bounded, which is new.** `macro:m = () { m!(); };` iterates
forever — the prototype fails to terminate on it, which is unacceptable in a
compiler under formal verification, where termination is itself a property to
establish (D-057).

## Work

1. The expansion pass itself, siting it **before name resolution** — expansion
   precedes every pass already built, and what the resolver sees is the expanded
   AST.
2. Parameter substitution into the body.
3. The fixed-point loop over module-level invocations.
4. **Two bounds, separately**, per the cycle README's recommendation: a depth bound
   on one invocation's nesting, and an iteration bound on the loop.
5. The diagnostic, which **names the macro and the chain that reached the bound** —
   "expansion limit exceeded" without the chain leaves the reader to find a cycle
   the compiler already walked.

## Why the bounds are two and not one

They fail differently. A deeply nested single expansion — `a!(b!(c!(…)))` — is a
program that is too complicated. Two macros expanding into each other is a program
that does not terminate. Reporting both as one budget makes the second look like
the first, and the fix for each is unrelated.

This is the same argument that gave the exhaustiveness rules three codes rather
than one (D-120): a reader told "not exhaustive" about an `int32` goes looking for
a variant they forgot.

## Watch for

**Spans come from two places after expansion.** A node in an expanded body was
written in the macro and instantiated at the call site, and a diagnostic pointing
at only one of those is pointing at half the story. Whether a span carries both is
a decision this subcycle has to make and 0.6.6 has to report against — the
alternative is every macro-related diagnostic in the language pointing into a
declaration the reader did not write.

**The AST grows during the walk.** Every existing pass walks a fixed tree; this one
appends while iterating. The scratch-window machinery (0.2.0) is what makes that
survivable, and it is worth checking that assumption early rather than at the
fixed point.

**`MacroPattern` is gone (D-057).** No `pick` arm matches an invocation, because by
the time a `pick` is analysed no invocation exists. `AST_REFERENCE.md` records the
removal; nothing here reintroduces it.

## Done when

- A macro invoked at module level expands, and one that expands to another
  invocation expands again.
- A macro that recurses without bound is refused, naming the macro.
- The depth bound and the iteration bound are separate, and refuse separately.
- Expansion runs before resolution, and every existing suite still passes over the
  expanded AST.
