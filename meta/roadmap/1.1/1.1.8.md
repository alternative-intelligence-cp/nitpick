# 1.1.8 — The executor (was slotted 1.1.5; moved by D-179)

Per the original plan, now built ON the typed-error substrate: the run
queue; futex parking on absolute monotonic deadlines
(FUTEX_WAIT_BITSET|CLOCK_MONOTONIC, pinned at 1.1.3); the windup SETTER and
the join deadline (D-083/D-176) — expiry sets every unfinished child's
windup word, the grace drive runs, a still-unfinished child traps
`DeadlineExceeded` (the CONSTANT now, not a bare −4107); `async main`'s
shim replaced by the real run loop; the D-063 stop-the-world hook.
Suspension becomes REACHABLE for the first time, which is what makes the
1.1.4 machines' re-entry paths runtime-testable — the resume switch, the
windup poll, and the park/wake protocol all get their first live coverage
here.

## Stages A+B outcome

**A** made suspension REACHABLE: `suspend_until(at)` is the language's one
waiting primitive (lowered inline — park request, state advance, `ret i8 1`,
resume switch landing after the call), `sleep(within)` is ordinary prelude
Nitpick over it, and the entry shim became a run loop. The first sleeping
program caught the futex op: 265 sets `FUTEX_CLOCK_REALTIME`, so a monotonic
timepoint read as realtime put every deadline in 1970 and returned instantly;
137 (`WAIT_BITSET|PRIVATE`, monotonic being the default) is correct.

**B** made D-071's property TRUE rather than aspirational. The frame header
gained `qnext` and `wake_at` (params moved to slot 9), the executor moved
into the runtime as a ready queue plus a sleepers list — both intrusive
through `qnext`, since a task is ready or sleeping and never both, and
`wake_at` is −1 once a task has completed so a join arriving late reads it
instead of waiting forever. `npk_step` is one step of the executor and
`npk_run_until(target, deadline)` is the shape both the entry shim (root)
and the join (each child) need; the deadlock, unwakeable-suspension and
post-trap freeze stops live in one place for every driver. A spawn enqueues
its child, so `executor_interleave.npk` proves three workers running
concurrently INSIDE their parent's own sleep.

Two corrections the tests forced, both now in the runtime's comments: the
executor's wait must be CLAMPED by the caller's deadline (without it the
loop sleeps to the earliest sleeper, far past a join deadline, and
containment silently becomes a wait — a 30-second child under a 120ms join
caught it), and the wind-up must WAKE a sleeping child (a flag read at the
next resume needs the resume to happen; due-ing it through the ordinary
path costs no list surgery). The grace is a stated 250ms rather than a
second full deadline — total containment `deadline + grace`, half the audit
surface.

**The wind-up's shape, settled by building it**: the poll lives at ONE site
— the suspension primitive — because every wait bottoms out there, so an
awaiting task learns through its awaitee's failure rather than a second
mechanism, and no frame is abandoned since each level unwinds in order. A
wait that should be windable writes `relay await`; `?|` swallows a wind-up
exactly as it swallows any error, which is uniform and is why the trap
exists beneath it. `executor_windup.npk` pins the cooperative unwind (the
parent relays `DeadlineExceeded`, main reads a `Result` error) and
`executor_windup_trap.npk` pins the backstop (a child that swallows and
sleeps on is trapped).

Remaining for a later subcycle: the per-thread move of the executor's
globals (1.1.9's `Thread.spawn` creates executors, and D-083 fixes each
one's deadline there), and channels (C-9).
