# 1.1.6 — The origin chain (D-179)

The failure path grows a diagnostic spine; the success path stays exactly
one zero store.

- The SITE TABLE: every `fail`/`relay`/`?!` site gets a compile-time
  interned id (module, function, line), emitted with the program.
- The RING: per-thread, fixed (8 sites + depth), allocator-free, in
  npkrt.ll beside the existing thread state. `fail` stamps-and-resets;
  every `relay`/`_^` hop appends; `?!` stamps before trapping.
- Prelude accessors so `failsafe` (and diagnostics) can read the chain.
- The JOIN re-stamps the winning child error at the join site — a parked
  error carries its code, not its chain (D-179: chains are diagnostics,
  codes are semantics; semantics are never truncated).
- D-080's amendment lands in the spec text: identity verbatim, chain grows.

Done when a three-hop relay shows three sites in order under a test that
reads the ring from `failsafe`, and a caught-then-refailed error shows a
reset chain.

## Outcome

Landed as planned, with three build-time findings worth keeping: the site
tables must emit at the program's TAIL (beside the declares they emitted a
one-row sentinel before any function had spoken); `npk_trap` must be
CHAIN-NEUTRAL (`?!` pushes and hands over an error whose history must
survive, so every trap CALLER stamps its own truth — guards and the
runtime's internal callers reset, `?!` pushes); and the guard family had
six sites, not three (two bounds shapes, tbb compare + cast, div zero and
div MIN). `fail` resets; `relay` and `?!` push; `!!!` and the emitted
guards reset at their own registered sites; the runtime's reserved site is
0; the coroutine join pushes the function's own site when it adopts a
child's error. `chain_depth`/`chain_site` are runtime-backed builtins;
`site_line`/`site_path` read per-program tables through emitted
bounds-checked lookups. origin_chain.npk (exit 42) pins the three-hop
relay chain, the `?!` hop surviving the trap, and the catch-then-fresh
reset. The 1.1.8 executor owes the ring its per-thread move.
