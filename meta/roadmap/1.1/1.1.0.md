# 1.1.0 — `never fails` on every function, checked; the statement forms closed (D-163)

**Cycle 1.1, opening.** The first third of D-163: the word gets its new home
and its check, the statement-side closed list lands, and the instrument that
measures how far the tree is from the licence is switched on. **`raw` and `drop`
are not yet refused** — that is 1.1.2's closing act, once the two sweeps have
driven the instrument's count to zero. Nothing in this subcycle changes an
emitted byte.

## Why first, and why here

- **Before 1.1 writes a line of the executor.** Everything this cycle builds —
  executors, channels, the Bridge — is thousands of lines of new `raw` and
  `drop`; written under the rule they need no second sweep.
- **1.1 already owns the word's fate.** D-149 retires `never fails` on `extern`
  declarations "landing with the Bridge work" (row 1.1.8). The keyword moves to
  its new home in the cycle that closes its old one.
- **1.1 owns the spawn form.** `drop work()` is the spawn construct (D-058), and
  rule 4's exception hands C-7 / C-9 the requirement that a spawned task's error
  reach the D-062 join. That requirement must be in force before the join is
  designed, not retrofitted.
- **The 1.0 call forms are built** — direct, UFCS, trait, `dyn` — so the licence
  has every callee shape to read and none to anticipate.

## What it builds

- **The grammar, one production wider.** `p_parse_failure`'s `never fails` arm
  is reachable from `p_parse_contracts`, so it lands in `FunctionDecl.contracts`
  as `VerifyKind.VerifyNeverFails` — a new verify kind, no new layout — on
  ordinary functions, trait method signatures, impl methods, and `comptime`
  functions. `parse_type.npk` accepts it after a function type's parameter list.
  `fails on` stays extern-only. `whole_grammar.npk` gains the three forms;
  `check_kinds_reachable` sees the kind.
- **The type.** `TY_FUNC` / `TY_FUNC_VARIADIC` carry a never-fails bit in the
  flags slot, part of identity (`tt_intern`, `func_types_equal`) and of the
  D-156 mangled name. `fn_type` sets it from the declaration; `fits` lets a
  marked function flow to an unmarked type and refuses the reverse
  (`TYPE_NEVER_FAILS_ASSIGN`).
- **The body check.** `fn_never_fails` on `ExprTyper`, set beside `fn_terminal`
  in `check_function`; read in `check_fail`, `check_return`, and the `relay` arm
  of `type_of_expr` (`TYPE_NEVER_FAILS_CAN_FAIL`, the message naming the
  statement). The same code refuses `requires` / `ensures` / `limit` on a
  marked function, the `async` modifier, and the clause on `main` / `failsafe`.
- **The statement-side closed list** (rule 6) — enforced now, because it
  refuses nothing the tree relies on except 188 bare statements, which this
  subcycle fixes: `check_stmt`'s expression-statement arm reads the expression's
  type and refuses a `Result` (`TYPE_RESULT_DISCARDED`), a non-`NIL` value,
  `raw` in statement position, and `discard(Result)`. The four value-less forms
  are accepted by shape. The 2 `src/` + 186 `tests/` bare calls
  (`wild_release_all();`, `dalloc(p);`, `a.destroy();` …) gain `drop` — which
  is licensed in 1.1.2, and until then is merely what they should have said.
- **The `defer` rule** (rule 7): `fail` and `relay` inside a `defer` body are
  refused (`TYPE_DEFER_EXIT`), with the backend's `run_defers_down_to` given a
  re-entrancy guard that reports an internal defect rather than recursing, in
  case a later bug reaches it.
- **Trait conformance.** `check_signature` / `same_signature` compare the
  contract as well as the types: an impl may not drop a trait method's
  `never fails` (`TYPE_NEVER_FAILS_IMPL`). `derive.npk` emits the clause the
  trait declares on every generated method.
- **Builtins.** `BUILTIN_REFERENCE.md` gains the `fails` column, filled by
  reading each bare-name builtin's IR body (`npkrt.ll` / the runtime floor), not
  its prose — OOM traps (D-150) and is not a `Result` error; an interior NUL
  (`to_cstring`) and every descriptor call are; the "→ void" builtins that trap
  on misuse (`dalloc`, `wildx_free`, `wild_release_all`) are `never fails`.
  `gen_tables.py` emits the column; the table is what the licence reads.
- **The seed learns the word.** `bootstrap/generator/parse.py` accepts
  `never fails` in the contract position of a function, a trait method, and a
  function type, **without setting `has_contract`** — `check.py` rung-refuses
  every other contract as 1.3 work, and this one changes no lowering. The seed
  also applies rule 6 to statements (it is what compiles `tests/` today).
- **The instrument: `check_raw_licensed`.** A whole-tree harness check in the
  `check_decisions_current` mould — **REPORTS, never fails** (in this
  subcycle): for each of `src/`, `lib/`, `tools/`, `tests/`, the count of `raw`
  and `drop` sites whose resolved callee does not carry the clause, split
  never-fails / may-fail / builtin, and the worklist by file. Resolution, not
  regex: it runs the real frontend (a `tools/` driver in the `parse_check` /
  `check` family) so UFCS and imports are answered correctly. Its first run
  replaces the heuristic's 262 / 741 / 868 with real numbers; those are the
  worklists of 1.1.1 and 1.1.2.
- **The decision and the docs.** D-163 into `DECISIONS.md`; the amendments
  its last section lists; `OPEN_DECISIONS` G-4 struck; the join's error
  requirement appended to C-7 / C-9.

## What it deliberately does not do

Refuse `raw` or `drop` on a callee without the clause. With the clause on
nothing yet, every one of the 7,900 sites is unlicensed, and a refusing checker
would fail the `selfhost` stage and every suite at once. The rule's *truth* is
1.1.2's closing commit; this subcycle makes it measurable and makes every claim
it will rest on checkable.

## Tests

`tests/types/rejection/never_fails.npk`: a marked function containing `fail`;
one containing `relay`; one containing `return Result{…}`; one with `requires`;
an `async` one; the clause on `main`; an impl dropping its trait's clause; a
may-fail function passed where `func int32(int32) never fails` is expected.
`tests/types/rejection/result_discarded.npk`: a bare `f();` on a `Result`
callee; `discard(f())`; `x + 1i32;`; `raw f();` in statement position; `fail`
and `relay` inside a `defer`. Each `expect-error` on its code and span.
`tests/types/accept/`: a marked function using `?`, `?!`, `is_err`, and
`pick … ERR:` over a may-fail callee (every permitted form, so over-refusal is
caught — the 0.5 lesson); `relay f();`, `f() ?! c;`, `f() ? NIL;` as
statements; a marked trait method with a marked impl; a marked function type
holding a marked function; a generic function marked `never fails` instantiated
twice; a `defer` handling a may-fail cleanup with `?!`.
`tests/conformance/`: a program with and without the clauses emitting
byte-identical IR.

## Done when

- Every wrong claim and every discarded `Result` in the rejection suites is
  refused on its code; every permitted form in the acceptance suite is silent.
- `check_raw_licensed` reports a number for each tree on every full run, and
  its worklists are committed as `meta/roadmap/1.1/raw_sweep_worklist.md`
  (the 0.8.0 pattern: the measured debt, classified, owned by the next two
  subcycles).
- The fixpoint holds byte-identical; `check_codes_tested`,
  `check_codes_centralised`, `check_kinds_reachable` are green with the new
  kind and codes.
- D-163 is in `DECISIONS.md` and every amendment it lists is made.

## Outcome

Everything the plan names landed, atomically, behind one green harness — the
grammar arm, the type, the body check, the statement closed list, the `defer`
rule, trait conformance, the seed's word and rule 6, the builtin `fails`
column, the instrument, and the sweep the statement rule forces.

### What the build looked like

- **`VerifyNeverFails`** is a generated kind (AST_REFERENCE §5 row →
  `gen_tables.py`), reachable from `p_parse_contracts` for ordinary functions,
  trait methods, impl methods and `comptime` functions, and after a function
  TYPE's parameter list (the node's spare payload slot carries the bit).
- **The never-fails bit rides the func type's `name` slot** — free on a
  function type, and already part of `tt_intern`'s identity key, so a marked
  and an unmarked type intern distinct with no new `Type` field.
  `func_never_fails` reads the slot via `tt_get` (not the `type_name`
  accessor — `check_identity_by_decl` rightly polices that one).
  `func_types_equal` stays shape-only; the CONTRACT is compared at the
  boundaries that own the direction — `fits` (marked→unmarked flows, the
  reverse refused), the two mismatch reporters (`TYPE-038` names the contract
  when the shapes agree), and `check_signature` (`TYPE-041` when an impl
  drops the trait's clause). The D-156 mangle picks the bit up through
  `type_display` (`… never fails`), so unmarked names — every pre-D-163
  symbol — are unchanged.
- **The checks**: `fn_never_fails` beside `fn_terminal` (`fail`, `relay`,
  `return Result{…}` → `TYPE-037`; `requires`/`ensures`, `limit` on the
  function or a parameter, `async`, the clause on `main`/`failsafe` → same
  code); rule 6 in the `StmtExprStmt` arm (`TYPE-039`: a bare call, a bare
  builtin call — which types as UNKNOWN, so it is refused by shape — a
  non-`NIL` value, `raw f();`, `discard(Result)`; the keyworded forms must
  themselves be `NIL`-typed, or the error is handled and the VALUE still
  discarded); rule 7 as `in_defer` (`TYPE-040`).
- **The seed** accepts `never fails` in the contract loop (no `has_contract` —
  it changes no lowering) and enforces rule 6 on statements, so the two
  frontends agree on what compiles. A seed-compiled frontend test uses the
  clause and `raw`s through it.
- **The backend is INERT**, now checked: the 1.3 contract rung skips
  `VerifyNeverFails` (found by the conformance twin, which refused before the
  fix), and `tests/conformance/nf_twin/` — two programs differing only in
  their clauses — must emit **byte-identical IR** on every run.

### The instrument's first numbers

`check --raw-report` (real resolution — the resolver's symbols and the
checker's recorded callees; 0 sites unresolved in `src/`) replaced the plan's
heuristic 262/741/868 with the real debt, committed to
`raw_sweep_worklist.md`:

| tree | may-fail | builtin |
|---|---|---|
| src | **8,921** | 100 |
| tests | 989 | 117 |
| prelude | 54 | 17 |
| tools | 22 | 3 |
| lib | 11 | 1 |

~10,000 sites against the heuristic's ~1,000 — the underestimate is exactly
why D-097 asks for measurement before belief. The sites concentrate onto far
fewer CALLEES (`ir_expr` alone holds 1,343), which is what 1.1.1 marks.

### The sweep, and what it taught

The statement rule refuses 193 bare statements the tree carried (`plan said
188`): `wild_release_all();` ×124, `dalloc(p);` ×35, the rest builtins and
arena methods. A line-regex sweeper applied `drop` — and the harness plus a
paren-balance audit caught THREE classes of sweeper bug before they could
land: a continuation line ending in bare `relay` (the insertion PARSED, as
`relay (drop …)` — the silent-corruption case), eight enum VARIANT
declarations (`Text(string);` line-matches a call), and four single-line
`defer { dalloc(p); }` bodies the line-shape missed. The arena-method
statements (`a.destroy();`) turned out to type as plain `NIL` — legal bare
under rule 6 — so the sweep there was reverted rather than kept: the closed
list is the CHECKER's law, not the sweeper's guess.
