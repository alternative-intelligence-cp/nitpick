# 0.5.2 — Definite assignment, `fixed`, and `defer`

**Cycle 0.5 — Static analyses.**

## Goal

Every binding is assigned before it is read, and an immutable one is assigned once.

## Work

1. **Definite assignment** — a read of a binding that no path assigned is refused.
   The merge at a branch is the whole content: assigned on one arm and not the
   other is **not** assigned.
2. **`fixed`** — an immutable binding may not be assigned after its initialiser.
   The qualifier parses today and the real frontend does not read it; `SUBSET_1`
   notes the seed enforces it, which means our own sources rely on a rule the
   compiler being built does not yet have.
3. **`const`** — the same shape at module scope, where the initialiser must also
   be constant.
4. **`defer` ordering** — a `defer` runs on normal exit paths and not on a trap
   (D-014). What this subcycle owes is the *order*: last registered runs first,
   and a `defer` may not reference a binding that is not assigned at the point it
   was registered.

## Why the merge is the whole content

Straight-line definite assignment is a loop over statements and is not where
analyses go wrong. **The merge is**: `if` with one arm assigning, a `while` whose
body may not run at all, a `pick` arm that `give`s and one that does not, a
`break` out of a loop that skipped the assignment.

An analysis right on straight-line code and wrong after a merge passes every test
written the easy way — which is precisely how 0.4.4's statement walk missed
sixteen kinds, all of them the ones that *contain* statements.

## Watch for

**A `while` body may run zero times.** Anything it assigns is not assigned after
the loop. The same is true of `if` with no `else`, and of a `pick` that is not
exhaustive — which is why 0.5.4 exists and why the two interact: an exhaustive
`pick` where every arm assigns *does* assign.

**`pass` and `fail` are exits.** A binding assigned only after an early `pass` is
not assigned on the path that took it.

## Done when

- Reading an unassigned binding is refused, including after a one-armed `if`.
- Assigning a `fixed` binding twice is refused.
- A binding assigned on every arm of an exhaustive `pick` is accepted after it.

---

## What this found

**D-116 came back, and the bug shipped again before it was caught.** The decision
said a binding-state analysis needs its body walked more than once, and named
definite assignment as one of the places it would recur. It recurred:

```nitpick
fixed int32:k;
while (c) { k = 1i32; }      // written once per iteration, which is once too many
```

A single walk from the loop's entry state sees `k` unassigned when `k = 1i32;` is
reached, and nothing fires. Fixed with a **silent pre-pass** over the body that
grows `may` before the reporting walk. One pre-pass suffices here where the borrow
marking needed an open-ended fixpoint, and the reason is worth keeping: `may` is set
by an assignment statement and by `$$m`, and **neither chains** — one walk visits
every assignment in the body whatever its order or nesting.

**Reading the decision was not enough to avoid writing the bug.** That is the more
useful finding: the shape has to be checked for deliberately at each analysis, not
recognised from having read about it.

## What turned out to be someone else's job

**`int32:x = x + 1i32;` is refused by the RESOLVER**, not by this analysis — the
name is not in scope until the declaration completes. Where an outer `x` exists,
the inner initialiser binds to that one, so there is no self-reference to catch
either way. The walk still reads the initialiser before marking the binding,
because that is the order the program has; it is just not load-bearing.

## Recorded as D-118

Two sets rather than one, the exit exemption, `$$m` assigning where `$$i` and `@`
read, `defer` checked at its registration, and `fixed` / `const` as one rule with
two spellings.

## Done when — all four met

- Reading an unassigned binding is refused, including after a one-armed `if`. ✓
- Assigning a `fixed` binding twice is refused, including once per loop iteration. ✓
- A binding assigned on every arm of an exhaustive `pick` is accepted after it. ✓
- `const` at module scope needs an initialiser, and a constant one. ✓

Verified by making it fail, four times: the merge as a union (12 findings, not 14),
the exit exemption removed (the acceptance suite refused), `$$m` treated as a read
(the endorsed out-parameter pattern refused), and the loop pre-pass removed (13
