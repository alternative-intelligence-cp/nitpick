# 0.2.1 — Parser core

**Cycle 0.2 — AST and parser.**

## Goal

A cursor over the `TokenList`, the expectation helpers every rule uses, and the
error-recovery strategy fixed before any rule depends on it.

## Recovery is a design decision, not a detail

A parser that stops at the first error reports one problem per run. Worse, a
parser that recovers *badly* reports a cascade of phantom errors after the real
one, which is more damaging than stopping -- the reader cannot tell which
diagnostic is the cause.

The strategy: **synchronise to a statement or declaration boundary.** On an
unexpected token, report once, then skip until a token that can legitimately
start a statement or declaration, or until a closing brace at the current depth.
Suppress further diagnostics until at least one token has been consumed
successfully, so one mistake cannot produce three messages.

## Work

1. `Parser` over a `TokenList`: `peek`, `advance`, `at`, `expect`.
2. Diagnostics through the `DiagList` from 0.0.6 -- codes, not message text.
3. Synchronisation, and the suppression rule above.
4. Depth tracking for brace, bracket and paren nesting.

## Done when

- `expect` reports a diagnostic with an accurate span and recovers.
- A file with several independent errors reports several, and no phantoms.
