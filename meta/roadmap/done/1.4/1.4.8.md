## Part A — the floor: `npk_spawn`

1. Generalize `npk_driver_clone_exec`'s proven shape
   (`runtime/npkrt.ll` post-1.4.6): same fork-shape `CLONE_PIDFD`
   clone, same allocation-free child (the copied-futex rule bars ANY
   Nitpick in the child), same PDEATHSIG + NO_NEW_PRIVS, same 16-slot
   registry (trap-route kill + the D-151/D-188 exit discipline extend
   to tool children). The generalization is the BLOCK: caller-directed
   stdio — [0]=path, [1]=argv, [2]=envp, [3..5]=the fds to dup3 onto
   0/1/2 (a pipe write-end for captured stdout, a file, or the
   inherited fd), [6]=optional ctrl fd for slot 3 (absent for tools),
   [7]=parent pid, [8..9]=OUT slot/pidfd. `spawn_driver` becomes a
   caller of the same primitive with its driver block (fd3 present,
   stdout→/dev/null preserved for drivers).
2. BUILTIN_REFERENCE gains the row (marked region, Signature + Fails
   columns per D-201's format); the generator regenerates; the builtin
   is table-typed from birth. Waiting stays `sys(WAITID)` from Nitpick
   (the nbridge pattern — reuse its constants and `fd_above_std`).
3. Runtime-floor test (the hand-IR stage) + a backend program spawning
   `/bin/true`-shaped fixtures... — NO: zero-dependency discipline —
   spawn the HARNESS-BUILT C reference driver? Also no: the clean
   fixture is npkc itself or a tiny compiled-by-npkc program from the
   fixtures dir (`tests/backend/fixtures/` precedent, `// argv:`
   substitution). Capture its stdout through the pipe; assert content.

### Decisions taken on contact (implementation-level; recorded so the reasons survive)

- **The primitive is the bare builtin `clone_exec`, floor symbol
  `@npk_clone_exec`.** D-206 wrote `npk_spawn`; `spawn` is the task keyword
  (D-062), so the bare name cannot be `spawn`, and a floor symbol that differs
  from its builtin's name for no reason would be an `ABI:` note carrying
  nothing. The old `driver_clone_exec` already said what the primitive does;
  the generalization drops the tenant from the name. `driver_retire`,
  `npk_driver_kill_all`, `npk_driver_live_count` and `DriverLeak` KEEP their
  names: the registry's original tenant is the driver, a tool child is
  supervised identically, and renaming an error identity costs every
  `failsafe` in the tree an arm for cosmetics.
- **The block is ten words and the descriptor rule is CHECKED in the
  runtime**, not trusted: `[3] [4] [5]` are the child's 0/1/2 sources,
  `[6]` the optional ctrl fd (negative = none), and a source below 4 (or a
  ctrl fd in 0..3) refuses `-EINVAL` before any slot is claimed. "Inherit our
  stdin" is spelled by dup'ing it above the std trio first, the same rule
  for every descriptor — one shape, no per-slot special case in the child.
- **`environ() → cstring[]` is a floor builtin**, measured once at `_start`
  beside `argv` by the one cstring-array builder (`npk_cstr_slice`, which
  now serves both). D-089 keeps `main`'s signature; the environment is the
  same boundary's other half and no syscall can return it, so it is the one
  piece of Part A the floor had to add. `npkg` reads `PATH` from it and
  passes it through to the tools it spawns.
- **The syscall vocabulary the Bridge and the tool runner share moves to
  `lib/nsys.npk`** — the x86_64 numbers, `fd_above_std`, `open_devnull`,
  the CLOEXEC-and-NONBLOCK `capture_pipe` — because REACH is import-scoped:
  `nproc` importing `nbridge` for six constants would make every Bridge
  error "reachable" in every build tool. `nsys` fails with the kernel's own
  errno (`fail r.err`, a dynamic operand REACH does not enumerate); the
  Bridge maps those to `EDriverSpawn` at its sites exactly as it did for its
  own copies.
- **`proc_wait` captures BOTH pipes and bounds every wait.** A child blocked
  on a full stdout while the parent waits on stderr is a deadlock, so the
  drain waits on whichever pipe is open (`io_ready2`), then on the pidfd for
  the exit; a deadline kills via the pidfd, reaps, retires, and fails
  `EProcDeadline`. Nothing in the runner ever blocks unboundedly on a live
  child.

### Step 2 — `TY_FLAGS` (D-230) — LANDED (2026-09-02)

The kind, the four families as keywords, the operator rule (`&`/`|` within one
family, `~` unary, `^` and the shifts refused, `NITPICK-TYPE-058`), the two
crossings, the B-7 sweep (seven excuse rows, nine walker arms), the lowering
pin, and the members as GENERATED `fixed` prelude bindings from
TYPE_REFERENCE §8's marked region — plus the user's families answer: `Whence`
stays, `Fcmd` and `Advice` are prelude enums, their kernel words mapped in
`nsys` (`fcntl_cmd`, `madvise_word`), and the Bridge's `fcntl` sites spell the
enum. Two things came up on contact, neither about flags:

- **An unchecked cast is its own node kind.** `=>!` parses as
  `ExprUncheckedCastExpr`, not `ExprCastExpr` with a flag, so the folder had
  never folded one — 1.4.7b's "TYPE-035 refuses a cast today" was this. The
  arm added folds `int32 =>! <flags>` and NOTHING else: an unchecked cast
  between numbers still does not fold, because "a cast keeps the value" is
  false for a narrowing and a folded 300 in an `int8` slot would be the
  silent wrong constant the folder exists to refuse.
- **`pub` on a module binding was never stored — and the export bit was read
  from the TYPE's node index.** A global declaration keeps its type in slot
  `a`, the slot `decl_flags` reads for every other declaration, and
  `p_parse_global` dropped the `flags` it was handed. So a `pub fixed`
  binding crossed a module boundary by the parity of its type's index (a
  probe with `pub fixed int32:GX` refused `GX` in the importer), and the
  same misread put a global's type index through `reject_wildx`'s flag test.
  Latent since module bindings landed (1.0.9d): no program had ever imported
  one, and the prelude's first — D-230's constants — were invisible to every
  program. Fixed at the source: a global's flags ride the high half of its
  qualifier slot, `decl_flags`/`decl_has` are kind-aware, and
  `global_export.npk` pins it. The dormant-rule pattern, again, and again
  found by making a rule apply rather than by a test of the rule.

A third thing was caught only by launching the harness, and it is the one
the record should be honest about: the prelude's new constants COLLIDE with the
int64 functions `nsys` (and the Bridge, and the mock driver) declared under the
same names — `O_RDWR`, `O_CLOEXEC`, `O_NONBLOCK`, `PROT_READ`, `PROT_WRITE`,
`MAP_SHARED` — and a program that declares a prelude name is an ERROR
(`bind_prelude_into`), so every Bridge and tool-runner test would have failed.
The order-of-steps note had put "the Bridge's colliding constants retire" in
step 4; the collision bites the moment the prelude declares the names, i.e.
here. Both runs were stopped by PID, the six declarations retired in favour of
the typed constants with the `=> int32` crossing at each `sys` register (the
wrappers' own rule now, exactly as D-044 wanted), and both runs restarted. The
quick belt that would have caught it before the launch — a Bridge test under
`quickemit`, not only the new programs — is now part of the step's checklist.

Noted, not acted on: `DECL_DISCARDED` and `DECL_THREAD` share the value 2048
in `parse_decl.npk`. They live in different slots of different node kinds (a
parameter's `c`, a function's `a`), so nothing collides today — but two names
for one bit in one family is the shape the next reader gets wrong.

Measured before the harness: `flags_basic.npk` 0, `global_export.npk` 42,
`ir_types.npk` and `resolve_types.npk` 0, `flags_rules.npk` exactly its ten
refusals at their lines, and the flags-aware compiler compiles `src/`.

10/64/25/19/7 rejections, 4 fixtures, 194 programs with the `-O2` leg, 2
runtime tests, 17 acceptance), exit 0, so D-151's leak check and D-188's
live-child check asserted a clean shutdown after ~1,000 supervised spawns.

Decisions taken on contact, each recorded where it lives:

- **The undefined-symbol scan reads the object itself** (`npkg/elf.npk`), not
  `llvm-readelf`'s text. The plan preferred spawning `llvm-readelf`; it is a
  fourth LLVM tool outside the `[toolchain]` pin whose output nothing checks,
  and the closed-world link is LAW (D-206 §4) — a law enforced by an unpinned
  tool's column layout is weaker than the rule deserves. ELF64's symbol table
  is thirty bounds-checked lines; the allowlist is derived from `npkrt.ll`'s
  `define`s plus `main` exactly as the harness derives it; and the harness
  still spawns `llvm-readelf`, so the parity stage cross-checks the two
  readers on every object it scans. BUILD_REFERENCE §4 carries the note.
- **D-004's conservative rule shaped the code, and the shape is recorded so the
  next tool does not rediscover it.** A call handed `@local` yields a
  borrow-tainted value whenever its result type can hold a pointer, and such
  a value can neither be returned nor stored in a field. The compiler's own
  idiom — tables passed down as parameters, borrow-derived values consumed in
  the frame that took them — is the only shape that fits, so: every read-only
  container goes BY VALUE (a `List<T>`, a `StrSet`, a `TomlDoc`, an `Expect`,
  a `ProcArgs` — none owns anything; the header copies, the block is shared);
  **`proc_wait` CONSUMES its `Proc`** (`move Proc:p`, `lib/nproc.npk`), because
  a `Proc->` borrow would taint the `ProcExit` it returns and no caller could
  pass the captured text up — after the wait the child is dead and reaped on
  every path, so nothing was left to hold anyway; and a `Run` embeds its
  `ProcExit` rather than moving fields out one by one, since moving one field
  moves the binding (MOVE-001). Two keyword collisions cost a build each:
  `fails` (from `never fails`) and `end`.
- **The manifest reader is strict** (`npkg/toml.npk`, `npkg/manifest.npk`):
  the TOML subset `nitpick.toml` is written in and nothing else, one schema
  with every key named, an unknown key refused by line, `edition` refused by
  name (D-077), `opt-level` held to 0 or 2 — the two flag sets `[toolchain]`
  pins (D-204). It lives beside the tool, not in `lib/`: the driver reads no
  manifest yet, and the day it does, this moves.
- **Parity is a harness stage, not a one-shot script**: `check_parity` builds
  `npkg` with the compiler under test, runs `npkg test --verdicts` from the
  manifest root, diffs the verdict lists (a unit one runner has and the other
  lacks, or judges differently, is a named failure) and byte-compares
  `build/npkc` with its own. Every per-file site in the harness now records a
  verdict (`--verdicts PATH` writes them), which is the first time the harness
  has said what it RAN rather than only what failed.
