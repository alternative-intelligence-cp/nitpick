# 1.0.4c — Overlap refusal and derive-on-generic (D-161's remaining clauses)

**Cycle 1.0.** The two clauses of D-161 that 1.0.4b did not meet, scheduled
here rather than left dangling. Both have a WRONG behaviour today, not an
absent one, which is why they are their own subcycle rather than a footnote.

## 1. Overlap is refused at the impl, not at the call

Today a family impl and a per-instance impl for one (type, trait) both collect,
and the program is refused only when something CALLS the method —
`NITPICK-TYPE-020`, "ambiguous method". Measured:

```nitpick
impl:<T>:Box<T>:S     = { func:w = int64(Box<T>:self)     { pass 8i64; }; };
impl:Box<int32>:S     = { func:w = int64(Box<int32>:self) { pass 4i64; }; };
// refused at `b.w()`, not at the second impl
```

TRAITS_REFERENCE §4.1 is unambiguous: *"There is at most one implementation of
a given trait for a given type. Overlapping implementations are a compile
error."* At the impl, not at the use. The consequence of the current shape is
concrete: a library containing an overlap that nothing inside it calls compiles
clean and hands the error to its consumer, at a call site in code that did
nothing wrong.

**What it builds.** A pass over the collected impl table refusing a
per-instance impl that a family impl of the same trait already covers — the
shape `check_coherence` already has (D-111's two-blanket-impls-of-one-trait
check is the same question one step more general). The diagnostic names both
impls, as the blanket-conflict one does.

**The rule to state while doing it**: D-064 §7 has no specialization, so
"family plus per-instance" is not "the specific one wins" — it is an overlap,
and both spellings are refused together.

**Blanket-versus-concrete is the opposite case, and stays that way.** §2.6 is
explicit that "a blanket impl beside a *concrete* one is not a conflict:
overlapping is the point", because D-111 defines the lookup ORDER — concrete
first, blanket second — so there is never a moment at which two candidates
exist and something has to choose. Nothing defines such an order for family
versus per-instance, and *giving* it one would be exactly the specialization
D-064 §7 rejected: `Box<int32>` would mean one impl or another depending on
what else was in scope, which is the blueprint rule's first facet broken. So
this subcycle refuses family-plus-instance and leaves blanket-plus-concrete
alone. `tests/frontend/type_generic.npk` k6 already pins the accepted side.

## 2. `#[derive]` on a generic subject

Today `#[derive(Eq)]` on `struct:Box<T>` synthesizes `impl:Box:Eq` — the bare
declaration with no arguments — and the author is shown three
`NITPICK-TYPE-016` "generic arity" errors at `<derived-1>:2`, a synthesized
file they never wrote. That is the worst available outcome: a wrong impl AND a
diagnostic pointing nowhere the reader can act on.

D-161 settles what to do: **synthesize the family form**
(`impl:<T: Eq>:Box<T>:Eq` — the element bound mirroring the derived trait),
**or refuse the generic subject by name** until that lands. With family impls
now real (1.0.4b), the synthesis is the honest target; the by-name refusal is
the fallback if the bound synthesis turns out to need its own decision.

**What it builds.** Either the family synthesis, or `derive` refusing a generic
subject with a message naming the declaration (never `<derived-N>`), plus a
`tests/derive/rejection/` case pinning whichever lands.

## Tests

- `tests/types/rejection/`: a family impl and a per-instance impl of one trait,
  refused at the second impl with both named — and NOT requiring a call to
  trigger it.
- `tests/derive/rejection/generic_subject.npk` (refusal) or a program deriving
  `Eq` on `Box<T>` and comparing two instances (synthesis) — whichever D-161's
  first clause resolves to.
- An acceptance case that a family impl and a per-instance impl of *different*
  traits on one type still coexist: overlapping is per (type, trait).

## Done when

An overlap refuses at the impl with both named and no call required;
`#[derive]` on a generic subject either works or refuses by name, and no
diagnostic points at `<derived-N>` for it; the fixpoint holds.

## What landed

**1. Overlap refused at the impl.** `check_coherence` in
`src/frontend/type_trait.npk` gained the family case. The two targets are
DIFFERENT interned types — `Box<T>` and `Box<int32>` — so the existing
`b.target == a.target` equality never saw them; `family_covers` asks the
question that matters instead: is either impl a family impl (not blanket, and
it declared parameters of its own), and do both targets share a DECLARATION —
which is what identity is (D-090), and what makes `Box<int32>` a member of
`Box<T>`'s family. `NITPICK-TYPE-013` now fires at the second impl **with no
call anywhere in the file**, which was the whole point: before this, a library
whose overlap nothing inside it called compiled clean and handed
`NITPICK-TYPE-020` to its consumer at a call site in code that did nothing
wrong.

**2. `#[derive]` on a generic subject synthesizes the family form.** A probe
settled which of D-161's two outcomes was available: a hand-written
`impl:<T>:Box<T>:Eq` type-checks, so synthesis was viable and the by-name
refusal fallback was not needed. `derive_one` now builds the impl head and the
type name separately — `head = "impl:<T>:"` and `tn = "Box<T>"` — from the
declaration's generic window, and all six generators take the head. The
non-generic path is unchanged (`head` stays `"impl:"`, `tn` stays the bare
name), and the two error helpers keep the BARE name, since a diagnostic saying
`Box` is what the reader wrote.

**No bound is synthesized, deliberately.** `T: Eq` looks right and is wrong: the
generated body compares fields with the OPERATOR (`!=`), never by calling a
trait method, so what it requires of `T` is what the operator requires — the
same rule that already let a derived `Eq` work on an `int32` field, which
implements no trait at all. A synthesized bound would refuse `Box<int32>` for
failing to implement a trait its own field type does not implement either.

**3. A 1.0.2b gap this surfaced, and closed.** The synthesized body hit
`NITPICK-RUNG-001`, and the cause was not in derive: generic-body **expression**
types stay unsubstituted. 1.0.2b substituted ANNOTATIONS through the resolver
scope, which is everything `identity<T>` has — but the checker walks a generic
body once with `T` opaque (D-107), so `self.v != other.v` reaches lowering
carrying a bare `T`, which has no representation. `LlCtx` gained the
specialization binding (`llctx_set_subst`), `ll_type`'s `TY_PARAM` arm resolves
a parameter to its argument while a specialization is being emitted, and
`emit_fn_instance` sets and clears it around the body. Outside a specialization
the arm still returns the rung, because there a parameter genuinely has no
representation.

**4. The diagnostic was wrong before it was ever printed.** The first version of
`family_overlap` bound its two halves to declaration ORDER — `check_coherence`
walks `i` then `j = i + 1`, so the earlier impl was described as the family and
the later as the member. Written the other way round (`impl:Box<int32>:S`
first), it would have told the reader that a per-instance impl "covers every
instance of the family". The roles are now read off the impls themselves, and
the both-families case (`Box<T>` beside `Box<U>`, which intern differently and
so reach the same check) has its own wording.

  another coexist, because overlapping is per (type, trait).
- `tests/backend/programs/derive_generic.npk` — `#[derive(Eq)]` on `Box<T>`,

- `tests/frontend/type_generic.npk` k6 — unchanged and still green: a blanket

- Full harness green: `types` 21 → 22, `programs` 60 → 61, and **stage 1

## Done when

An overlap refuses at the impl with both named and no call required;
`#[derive]` on a generic subject either works or refuses by name, and no
diagnostic points at `<derived-N>` for it; the fixpoint holds. **Met** — and
the derive clause landed on the better of the two branches, synthesis rather
than refusal.

## What this subcycle found, and who owns it

**Nothing prints a diagnostic's message.** Verifying item 4 above meant reading
the wording out of the compiler — and there is no way to, because
`Diagnostic.message` is written at 79 sites in `src/` and read by nothing that
runs. Both drivers define their own `emit_line` printing `CODE path:line:col`;
the one renderer that keeps the message (`diag_render`) drops the file path and
is called only by `tests/frontend/diagnostics.npk`. The role-reversal bug above
is the direct consequence: an unprinted message is an unreviewed message, and
all 79 are in that state.

**Owner: 1.0.8**, scheduled at the tail of this cycle rather than filed against
1.2's `npkg` output work, because the cost of waiting is measured in unreviewed
message text and it grows every cycle.
